Owner: Isla. Reader: Justin, one page, once a week.
Started 2026-09-16. Structure borrowed from Alex Hormozi's Scaling Roadmap (stage,
constraint, plays, graduation checklist). Numbers and channels are ours, not his.

**Everything marked ⚙ is Isla's starting guess. Justin changes it by saying so.**

## 1. Stages

Stages are defined by AUM because that's the scoreboard Justin already runs the
practice on, and the marketing budget formula (10 bps of year-end AUM) hangs off it.
A stage has one constraint. Plays attack the constraint. You graduate when the
checklist is true, not when the AUM number is hit — the number is the band, the
checklist is the gate. Each graduation unlocks the next director from CLAUDE.md's
"Not yet built" list. Nobody builds a director early.

### Stage A — Advertise ⚙ $20M to $35M AUM ← **we are here (~$22M)**

**Constraint:** top of funnel. One lead input (mailer → class), twice a year, and
that input is saturating. Between classes the input is zero.

**Plays:**
- The spring mailer + class (keep; one per year, funded first).
- The mirror: FB test first, then mailed card if the mechanism shows up.
- The correction-triggered email (standing play, fires on the trigger).
- The referral ritual (Sloan drafts; it's the warm channel at scale).
- The stay-warm cadence for the $7–10M class pipeline (Sloan drafts the copy).
- YouTube live-builds as the always-on version of the class.

**Graduate when all of these are true:**
- [ ] Two lead sources other than the class each produced buying units in the
      trailing 12 months, on their own.
- [ ] The Core Four (section 2) ran for 12 consecutive weeks at or above count.
- [ ] The marketing budget formula held for one full year (spent the plan, not the
      panic).
- [ ] A piece went from Sloan → Ed → Justin's sign-off → published without Isla
      having to chase any step.
- [ ] Justin can name, from the scoreboard, which channel each new household
      came from.

**Unlocks on graduation:** Planning & Advisory support (meeting prep, follow-up
tracking). Not before — see the caution in section 5.

### Stage B — Capacity ⚙ $35M to $60M AUM

**Constraint:** Justin's calendar. At ~90% conversion, marketing that works turns
into meetings that don't fit. The bottleneck moves from "not enough people" to
"not enough Justin" fast — possibly inside one good year.

**Plays:** meeting prep built by an agent, follow-up tracking, service checklists
(rollovers, account opening), a written first-meeting agenda that enforces the
no-pitch rule without Justin remembering to.

**Graduate when:**
- [ ] Prep and follow-up for every first meeting happen without Justin doing them.
- [ ] A typical week is mostly client-facing hours; admin is measurably down
      (Justin sets the number ⚙).
- [ ] Service requests have one tracked owner and a status.
- [ ] The Core Four is still running at count — growth didn't stop to fix capacity.

**Unlocks:** Client Service & Implementation, then Growth & Pipeline (the pipeline
gets one owner instead of being split).

### Stage C — Leverage ⚙ $60M to $100M AUM

**Constraint:** capacity no system fixes. One advisor has a ceiling.

**Plays:** a second human (associate advisor or paraplanner), institutional 401k
plan-sponsor outreach, the Medicare/ACA line as a real channel, Market & Policy
Research so Justin stops reading regulation at night.

**Graduate when:** $100M is the goal, not a stage. Define this one when Stage B is
real; anything written now would be fiction.

## 2. The Core Four — Justin's version

Hormozi's rule is a hundred of each, daily. Ours is not, on purpose: a hundred cold
reach-outs a day from a licensed adviser to $250K households is a compliance
problem (TCPA, do-not-call, Ed's whole world) and a brand problem (Godin:
interruption). The number that matters is that every input is nonzero every week
and counted. Inputs equal outputs. Zero input weeks are the actual failure mode.

| Input | Ours | Weekly count ⚙ | Owner | Compliance note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm reach-outs** | Personal touches to clients, class attendees, the class pipeline, CPAs/attorneys. The correction email and stay-warm cadence are this at scale. | 25 | Justin (Sloan drafts the cadence) | Existing relationships; TCPA consent status still gates texts |
| **Content** | YouTube live-builds; clips and posts repurposed from them | 1 long-form / 2 weeks; 3 clips / week | Sloan → Ed | Every piece through Ed before publish |
| **Cold at scale** | The spring mailer, the mirror card, venue/union/credit-union outreach for new class hosts | 3 venue contacts / week; mailers by season | Sloan (venue-partner-researcher) → Justin confirms before any real venue is touched | Confirm before touching a real external relationship |
| **Paid** | The FB mirror test; later, whatever earns it | Spend and bookings logged weekly; no minute count | Justin runs the account; Sloan builds; Ed clears | Special Ad Category; OH/KY geofence; Calendly spec |

Why no minutes-per-day column: Justin's time is the Stage B constraint. Counting
his minutes on marketing now would optimize the wrong thing. Count outputs.

## 3. Cadence

**Weekly (Justin, 5 minutes; Isla prepares):** the scoreboard row below gets filled.
Four input counts, bookings, first meetings held, units closed, AUM added. Isla
flags any input at zero in plain words. Nothing else.

**Monthly (Isla reports, Justin reads):** what moved, what didn't, one decision
Justin needs to make. Ed's log of what published. Spend against the budget formula.
No transcript, no agent-by-agent recap.

**Quarterly (graduation check):** walk the current stage's checklist. Each box is
true or not — no partial credit. If the constraint has visibly moved (Justin's
calendar is full and the funnel isn't the problem), say so even if the AUM band
hasn't been reached. The checklist outranks the band.

**Standing triggers, outside the cadence:** market correction (S&P 500 closes 15%
off its trailing 52-week high) fires the correction email play, once per episode.
Class season fires the mailer play. These don't wait for a monthly.

## 5. Honest notes

- **Hormozi's constraint moves slower than ours.** His stages assume volume is the
  lever and conversion is the problem. Justin's conversion is ~90%. The moment
  Stage A works, Stage B's constraint arrives. Plan the Stage B build during Stage
  A, don't start it after.
- **Don't build the director before the constraint.** Planning & Advisory support
  will feel useful today. It isn't the bottleneck today. Building it now splits
  attention from the one thing that is.
- **A graduation criterion has to be measurable quarterly.** "Two sources produced
  units" is. "Brand is stronger" isn't. If a box can't be checked with a number or
  a yes/no, it doesn't belong on the list.
- **The stage bands are guesses.** Justin should move them. What shouldn't move is
  the rule that the checklist, not the band, is the gate.
- **This file is the operating plan the whole team works inside.** Sloan's queue,
  Ed's log, Taleb's reviews, and Isla's reports all point back here. When Justin
  asks "what should we be doing," the answer is the current stage's plays and the
  Core Four counts, not a new idea.
