## The honest tension he names against himself

**The only peer-reviewed January mechanism is the fresh-start effect** (Dai, Milkman & Riis,
*Management Science*, 2014 — grade B, search summary only): searches for "diet," gym visits
and goal commitments rise after temporal landmarks. **That is aspirational behaviour. So the
evidence for January supports the framing we rejected.** He chooses relief anyway, and says
plainly that he is giving up the only evidenced mechanism because the aspirational version is
the crowd.

## What a 5,000-piece January drop can actually read

Base B1: **1 booked household per ~5,800 pieces** (April 2026's 1-per-3,866 attendee-units
÷ 1.5; the 1.5 is the 11-individuals-across-7-households reconciliation — plausible, not
verified, 2021 vintage only). At 5,000 pieces, **0.86 expected bookings.**

| True rate vs base | Expected | P(≤1) | P(≥5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0x | 0.86 | 79% | 0.2% |
| 2x | 1.72 | 49% | 3.1% |
| 3x | 2.59 | 27% | 12% |

**A January lift under 2x is invisible. A result of ≤1 rules out only ~5.8x, not 2x or 3x.**

**So: the 2026 drop is not an experiment on January. It is rent with instruments, plus a
forcing function.** Its durable output is **the machine** — Calendly, the no-pitch note, the
four instruments, a Q5-cleared mirror — **and that machine is the same readiness the held
pool needs.** That is the reason to mail rather than skip.

   print gate for an April drop. Workable, tight, and season-confounded.
4. **Commit before knowing.** The December invoice precedes any knowledge of January mailbox
   density or 2026's year-end return.

## What the January piece is about: the envelope, not the calendar

**The subject is the year-end statement, and the question every household asks opening one:
"is this fine?"** A statement reports what you hold; it does not show how the pieces fit. The
mirror answers that privately, with the statement beside you and nobody to hand it to —
**which inverts "bring your statements,"** already flagged in the record as friction.

**Collapse risk:** "what your statement doesn't show" is the hidden-fee and
fear-of-your-advisor staple. Two guards: **the subject is composition, not fees**, and **the
statement is a prop, not a villain.**
