## First: this isn't a shorter version of v5. Here's what it actually is.

**Format: a two-sided postcard, not a bi-fold.** Not a trim of the four-panel spec —
a different object.

Here's the reasoning, answering the brief's three questions directly:

- **Does Panel 2 survive, doing a different job?** No. Once the confession is gone,
  there's no panel left to survive — there's one short line of credibility, and it
  belongs next to the offer, not on its own panel.
- **Do panels 2 and 3 merge?** Functionally yes — but calling it "panels 2 and 3
  merged" undersells it. What's left is a single reason-to-believe line plus the
  offer plus the RSVP, which is naturally one continuous read, not two headlines
  glued together.
- **Does it stop being a bi-fold?** Yes, and I think it should. A bi-fold that opens
  to reveal 60-odd words and a lot of white space reads as an underfilled brochure —
  the fold itself promises there's something on the other side, and finding almost
  nothing there undercuts a lean piece more than a card would, where the same amount
  of copy looks like a deliberate choice instead of a piece that ran out of things to
  say. A card also removes a friction step the fold piece has and this one doesn't
  need: **nothing is hidden behind an unfold.** Everything the piece has to say is
  visible the moment it's picked up and flipped — a lower-friction action than opening
  a folded self-mailer, and appropriate to a piece this short.
- **The cost argument, which I'd make even without the friction point:** a card is
  cheaper to produce than a folded self-mailer — no fold operation, likely lighter
  stock, possibly a lower postage class. Isla's point stands and I'd take it further:
  **cheaper-per-piece is the actual lever for a piece built to be Cell B of a test.**
  If Cell A and Cell B are being compared on cost-per-outcome (see below), Cell B
  should be the cheap arm on purpose — that's what makes the comparison informative
  rather than just "shorter version of the same spend."

**Format spec:** roughly 6×9 jumbo postcard (large enough to carry the full Block A
disclosure legibly on the back — see the production flag below), no envelope, front
and back both printed.

**Visual direction, carried forward unchanged from v5:** front is type only, black on
cream or white stock, no photo, no illustration — same reasoning as v5 (keeps the
piece off the tasteful-brand-essay and sailboat-aspiration failure modes, and Jayme's
own best covers were frequently pure type). The Financial House stays off this piece
for the same reason it stayed off v5 — a teaching aid, not a hook, belongs where
someone's already agreed to be taught.

**Photo, moved and re-justified, not just carried over.** v5 put Justin's photo on
Panel 2, doing "personal-register, earns its place next to the confession" work.
There's no confession here for it to sit next to. I'd put it on the back instead,
near the RSVP block — its job changes from *credibility through history* to
*presence*: this is a real person, he'll be the one standing in the room, not a
stand-in. That's a smaller claim than v5's photo was making, and an honest one.

## Why I removed the insider material entirely, not just to one line

This is the part of the brief I'm pushing back on, so I want to show the reasoning
rather than just report the outcome.

**The instruction assumed a one-line version of the insider material would give
Justin "a version that ships today" — I don't think it does.** The boundary map's own
logic is that the Portfolio Medics gate is triggered by *narrating the broker/IAR
distinction and Justin's own transition* — not by length. The cleared one-line
version I drafted first (below, kept as the alternative) still does that narrating,
just briefly. If a single sentence about being a broker then becoming an IAR still
counts as "narrating the transition," then a one-line Cell B doesn't actually dodge
the PM gate — it just makes the wait for PM clearance apply to a shorter piece,
which isn't the same as a piece that "needs no Portfolio Medics review... at all," as
the brief frames it. I'd rather tell Justin that plainly than hand him a piece that
looks gate-free and isn't.

**So the version that actually ships today has to contain zero sentences about
Justin's registration history** — not a short one, none. That's a bigger cut than
the brief asked for, and I want to be direct about why I think it's still the right
call, on the merits, not just as a technicality:

**The confession is reasoning for someone already leaning in.** v5's own Panel 3 note
says the mailer's only job is the invitation — the class does the persuading. A cold
stranger deciding whether to RSVP doesn't need Justin's registration history to make
that decision; they need the hook and the offer, stated as clearly as possible.
Explaining *why* Justin is credible, in a stranger's three seconds, is itself a
version of "argument as layout" — it assumes a reader who's already decided to keep
reading, which on a cold card, they haven't yet. That's the same reasoning the whole
practice already applies to buckets; I think it applies to biography too, at this
altitude, harder than I expected when I wrote v5.

**What replaces it: boundary-map item 8, used as written, which does a different kind
of credibility work — about the piece, not about Justin's history.**

> *"You've probably had a dinner-seminar mailer like this one in your stack before.
> This isn't that."*

Cleared by Ed "as written." Says nothing about Justin's registration history, so it
carries none of the U5-date, "alongside"-employer, or wholesaler-registration
dependencies that the three still-open consistency items attach to — and it doesn't
touch the PM-gate question at all, because it never narrates a transition. It also
doesn't name Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James, or any firm, so v5's flag #3 (whether
the class deck's PM clearance travels to mailed print) doesn't apply here either.

**The alternative I didn't pick, kept visible on purpose:** the compact, pre-cleared
line from the boundary map's own "Clears" example —

> *"I spent years as a broker before becoming a fee-based investment adviser
> representative — and there's a real difference in what each one is required to put
> first."*

This is real, unique, hard-to-copy credibility — no other advisor mailing into these
ZIPs can say it — and I don't think cutting it is free. If Justin wants it back, this
exact sentence is the lowest-risk way to do it: it's already given as a cleared
example, not new construction. But I believe (not confirmed by Ed) it still trips the
PM-review requirement by the boundary map's own stated logic, so restoring it likely
also restores the wait. That trade is Justin's to make with the timeline in front of
him, not mine to make silently by picking the version that's easiest to defend.

## What I'd expect this to beat, and what I'd expect it to lose to, against Cell A

**Where I'd expect Cell B to win:** cost-per-piece (no fold, likely lighter stock),
which at a fixed budget means more volume or more frequency for the same spend; and
read-completion — there's no unfold barrier and less to read before the ask, so a
higher share of people who pick it up should get to the RSVP line. If the cover is
doing the real work (my belief, not yet tested), a piece that gets out of the cover's
way faster should convert what the cover generates more efficiently, piece for piece.

**Where I'd expect Cell B to lose:** if the confession is actually contributing
persuasion beyond what the cover alone produces — not just reason-to-believe in the
abstract, but the specific, hard-to-copy fact that Justin used to fund the machine
he's now telling a stranger not to over-rely on — then Cell A should out-convert on a
per-piece basis, because Cell B has nothing structurally unique behind the cover; the
"this isn't that" kicker differentiates the piece from the genre but says nothing a
competitor couldn't also print. That's the actual question a real test would answer,
and it's the same question the original two-cell proposal was built to answer — I've
just now built both cells instead of describing one of them.

**Sample-size caveat, stated once so it doesn't get skipped later:** at the volumes a
first test is likely to run, a response-rate difference between these two cells inside
a percentage point is probably noise, not signal. What a modest first run can read
reliably is **cost-per-RSVP by cell** and **cost-per-attended-class by cell** — not a
confident verdict on which persuasion approach is "better" in the abstract. If Justin
wants a real read on the confession's marginal value specifically, that likely needs
either a larger run or a longer measurement window than a first test will have.

## The straight answer to Isla's question

**If Portfolio Medics cleared tomorrow and timing stopped mattering, would I still run
this version first? Split answer, because the brief bundles two decisions that
deserve different answers.**

**The format — yes, unconditionally.** The postcard argument doesn't depend on
timing at all; it's a cost and friction argument that holds regardless of when
Portfolio Medics answers. I'd build this as a card even in a world with no compliance
gate anywhere in it.

**The insider-material cut — no, not blind.** If timing were free, I wouldn't default
to zero insider material just because zero is easiest to clear. I'd want to actually
test whether the confession earns its word count, which means I'd want Cell A built
and mailed alongside something closer to it, not alongside a version I built partly to
dodge a gate. **So this specific piece, as drafted, is substantially a timing hedge —
say that plainly to Justin rather than let it read as my confident first choice.**
It's a real piece I'd defend on its own terms (the argument-as-layout point about
biography is one I believe, not just a excuse), but if I'm honest about why it looks
exactly like this, timing is a real part of the answer, not just the format decision.
