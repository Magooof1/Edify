## The question put to Sloan

The land has two possible payoff paths:

1. **Consumer discovery** — a near-retiree watches, eventually books. Slow, one at a time.
2. **B2B credentialing** — an HR director, plant manager or owner sees Justin can teach
   and invites him in to educate employees. One video produces a room.

Can one body of content serve both audiences, or does serving both serve neither?

## Sloan's answer: one content line. No employer-facing pieces.

Build path 1 exactly as designed. Let path 2 ride on it through a thin discovery wrapper
(a description line, a pinned comment) costing zero camera minutes.

**Why one asset genuinely serves both, rather than this being a hedge.** The two
audiences don't compete for the same real estate because they aren't evaluating on the
same axis:

- The **58-year-old** asks *does this apply to me, do I feel understood.* An
  identification check.
- The **HR director** asks *can this guy actually teach, and will he behave in my
  building without pitching my employees.* A competence-and-safety check.

**He doesn't need the video to speak to him. He needs to watch Justin be good at the
thing, on someone else.** That's demonstration content for a B2B buyer — the way a
consultant wins corporate work with a reel of solving a *client's* problem, not a reel
titled "for facilities managers."

A video gesturing at both — the retiree as hero plus an implicit wave to the employer —
breaks StoryBrand's central discipline: **one hero per story.** Build for the retiree
fully, and the tape persuades the employer as a side effect of being genuinely good.

## Why she rejects "a few pieces aimed at employers" outright

Not a lighter version of the same series — **a second content line wearing the same
channel's skin.** Employer-facing work needs different evidence (liability, retention,
absenteeism, fiduciary exposure), which means a different script genre: closer to a
pitch than a class. **That is the steak-dinner energy Justin rejected on principle,
aimed at a gatekeeper instead of a prospect.**

Three concrete objections to building it now:

1. It forks Justin's fixed camera-time budget toward a payoff with **no defined signal**.
2. It designs an "invite me into your workplace" message before the PTE 2020-02 /
   rollover-exposure question has been so much as scoped with Ed.
3. **It is diffusion in month one**, against a decision made specifically to prevent
   diffusion. The newsletter is parked for the same reason.

## Two corrections to Isla, both accepted

### 1. The predict-then-reveal format did not "close the hole." There was no hole.

Isla claimed Sloan's sample-couple rhythm rescued the Socratic mechanism on camera.
Sloan's correction, which is the accurate one:

**The chorus exists in the room to provide anonymity** — "spoken aloud but nobody can
tell who said what" — because a room contains other people to be embarrassed in front
of. **A solo viewer has no audience to be exposed to.** Video doesn't inherit the room's
solution; it never needed one.

The rhythm *is* genuinely video-native. The safety mechanism is simply moot.

**What video plausibly loses instead, which neither Isla nor Taleb had:** the ambient
*"it's not just me"* — watching twenty other people guess wrong together. That is closer
to what Justin's own account describes: the feeling surfacing among other confused
people, not alone. **Worth testing, not asserting either way.**

### 2. The dry run does NOT test two formats at once

Isla told Justin twice that the free-audience class dry run would validate both the room
format and the future video format. **It doesn't.**

A recorded room event and a from-scratch solo-camera build are different artifacts. In a
room Justin is unconsciously performing against laughter, restlessness and an audible
chorus — none of which exists alone in front of a lens.

**The dry run validates the beat sequence and Justin's pacing. It does not validate that
a solo cut carries the same rhythm.** Separate test.

## ISLA'S ADDITION — the discovery chain nobody priced

The demo-reel logic works **when the buyer can find the reel.** An HR director in
Covington is not searching YouTube for retirement educators. Path 2 depends on a thin
chain: an employee finds it and forwards it, or someone happens to search.

**So the honest version of Justin's idea is narrower than "the content gets me in the
back door."** His words were *"without the bureaucracy of going head to head with other
advisors"* — and that part holds. But:

> **The content doesn't replace the approach. It makes the approach land.**

He still has to knock. He arrives with something to show rather than a phone call, which
is the difference between a cold pitch and a warm demonstration — and that is worth a
great deal. It is not the same as being found.

## DECIDED BY JUSTIN, 2026-09-25 — all five

1. **Solo camera.** Not a recorded room. Controllable, re-shootable, scales; no releases,
   no class date, no one-off.
2. **Path 2's signal: one unprompted inbound inquiry from an employer within twelve
   months.** Parallel to path 1's booked-meeting-at-zero-spend.
3. **"Not a real couple" stands as the disclaimer shape** — Ed still supplies exact
   wording.
4. **No employer-facing language anywhere** — description, pinned comment, channel about
   — until Ed has scoped the PTE 2020-02 / employer-education question.
5. **Sloan's costless side door is approved.** One description line; the discipline is
   "one asset" in production, not in metadata.

**A consequence of (1) worth holding onto.** Justin also declined the class dry run (see
`drafts/class-sample-couple-build-v1.md`). Those two answers combine better than either
alone: **filming solo is itself the rehearsal.** A retake costs a few minutes; a live room
cannot be re-run. The format now gets seasoned in front of a lens, where mistakes are
free, before it is ever performed in a room where they aren't.
