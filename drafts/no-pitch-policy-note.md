**Purpose of this document.** This note substantiates, for Trulip's own compliance
file, the factual claim made in printed and mailed marketing that a first visit with
Justin is free, conversational, and does not involve a product pitch, application, or
signature. Under Advisers Act Rule 204-2, a factual claim about how the business
operates that is printed and distributed — particularly at volume, to a cold list —
needs a record substantiating it that exists before the claim goes out, not merely

document, not marketing copy, and is not intended for external distribution except in
response to a regulatory request.

## 1. What the first visit is

- **Format:** a one-on-one, in-person conversation. Justin's own description, verbatim
  from the class script: *"just going to be you sitting on a couch, having a
  conversation, asking those questions."*
- **Duration:** approximately one hour (the Step 2 Form labels it a "1 Hour Visit").
- **Location:** Trulip's office in Crestview Hills, Kentucky (behind Thomas More
  University). [BLANK — the Step 2 Form also lists "Harrison" as a location option for
  class-connected visits. Confirm whether a Harrison location is ever used for a first
  visit originating outside the class, or whether Crestview Hills is the only location
  used for mail/web-originated visits.]
- **Cost:** no cost to the attendee. Stated on the Step 2 Form as *"No Cost (Included as
  part of class)"* and by Justin in the script as *"it's free, it's included as part of
  the class."*

**Confirmed 2026-09-22 by Justin.** *"Yes the cold visit is the same, no form though."*
A first visit booked directly from mail or web (a cold lead who has never attended a
class) is the **same meeting** as a class-connected Step 2 visit — same hour, same
location, same content, same boundaries (Sections 2–4 below apply identically to
both entry points). The only operational difference is that a cold-booked attendee
arrives without a completed Step 2 Form, which changes how the agenda is set — see
Section 2.

## 2. What happens at the first visit

- The agenda is driven by the attendee's own stated areas of concern, not by Justin's
  selection. For a class attendee this is the checkbox section of the Step 2 Form —
  Foundation (Investments), Income, Legacy, Health & Care — checked by the attendee
  before the visit is scheduled, so the agenda is set before Justin sits down.
- **Confirmed 2026-09-22 — the cold-lead case.** For a visit booked directly from mail
  or web, there is no completed Step 2 Form in hand. The visit instead opens with
  Justin asking what brought the attendee in, and the agenda is established verbally
  at the start of the conversation rather than pre-selected by checkbox. This is
  deliberate on the marketing side, not an operational gap: the booking page and
  mailer carry no concern checklist by design, because a list of worries reads
  naturally on a form filled out after three hours in a room, and reads like a
  dossier when a stranger is asked to fill one out cold before ever meeting him. The
  "attendee sets the agenda" claim holds for both entry points; only the mechanism
  differs — checkbox in advance versus spoken at the start.
- Justin discusses the checked/stated areas of concern in conversation. Per the script,
  this includes questions such as: how much Social Security the attendee has and when to
  take it, how a pension coordinates, what monthly expense need exists and whether
  inflation-adjusted, and whether there are gaps between income and expenses.
- If the attendee has brought documents (see Section 4 below), Justin reviews them in
  the visit to answer the question "what do you already have" — see Section 4 for why
  this is diagnostic rather than a sale.

## 3. What does not happen at the first visit

Stated here as policy, not aspiration:

- **No specific product is recommended.** No insurance product, annuity, or investment
  product is proposed or discussed as something the attendee should purchase.
- **No application is taken and nothing is signed.** The first visit does not include
  paperwork of any kind that commits the attendee to anything.
- **No solicitation.** The visit is not used to solicit the sale of an insurance or
  investment product.

**CONFIRM OR CORRECT.** As drafted, this section states the boundary as a hard line:
nothing named, nothing proposed, nothing signed. If practice actually has a softer
edge — for instance, a general observation spoken out loud about a category being
unprotected or underfunded, offered without naming a specific product or making a
specific recommendation — say so directly, and this list should be corrected to state
that edge as policy rather than leave it unstated. Better that a line here gets struck
or narrowed than that this document states a tighter boundary than what actually
happens in the room.

## 4. What happens when an attendee brings documents

The class script and the mailer both tell the attendee to *"bring whatever's most
comfortable for you"* — including nothing. When an attendee does bring account
statements or other documents to a first visit, the following applies as policy:

- Documents are reviewed **only** to answer the diagnostic question of what the
  attendee currently holds and how it maps to the three categories Justin uses
  (Principal Protected / Manage Risk — Tactical Investments / Accept Risk — Buy and
  Hold Investments, per the Step 2 Form's own labels).
- This review is **not** a recommendation, proposal, or sales presentation. Identifying
  what an attendee already owns is not the same act as recommending what they should
  own, and this policy treats the two as categorically separate: the first happens at a
  first visit, the second does not.
- No product is proposed based on what the documents show, at the first visit.

**CONFIRM OR CORRECT.** Same principle as Section 3 — this is one continuous boundary
with a document review on one side of it and a product conversation on the other. If
reality has a soft edge here too, correct it here directly rather than leaving this
section cleaner than the practice it's meant to substantiate.

## 5. What diagnosis is possible depending on what's brought

The mailer promises: *"We'll look at what you've already got and tell you how much of
it is green, how much is blue, and how much is red."*

**Resolved 2026-09-22.** Justin approved restoring his own class line to the mailer,
which a prior draft had kept the first half of and cut the second: *"Bring whatever's
most comfortable for you. The more you bring, the more precise we can be."* Jayme is
applying that fix to the mailer's copy. Policy, stated to match:

- **Without documents,** the first visit still produces a real conversation about the
  green/blue/red categories and a rough read of the attendee's mix, built from what
  the attendee describes and knows about their own accounts.
- **With documents,** the same conversation produces a materially more precise
  breakdown, because Justin is looking at actual holdings rather than a description of
  them.
- Neither case involves a product recommendation — see Sections 3–4.

The promise scales with what's brought; it is not identical either way, and the
mailer's copy should read that way once Jayme's fix is in.

## 6. What triggers a second visit, and who decides

Justin's own words from the script: *"if you decide to bring some stuff and it makes
sense, we'll get back together for a second visit."*

**CONFIRM OR CORRECT — Ed's reading, not yet Justin's own words.** The phrasing reads
as a joint call reached in the room during the first visit's conversation: the
attendee has said or shown enough (whether from documents brought or from what they've
described) that a second visit is a reasonable next step for both of them, and Justin
proposes it there rather than deciding afterward on his own. On this reading, a second
visit is not automatic just because documents were brought, and it isn't the
attendee's unilateral choice either — it's Justin's professional judgment, offered and
agreed to in the conversation itself. Correct this directly if it's wrong.

The remaining sub-question: what happens to an attendee who brings documents but for
whom a second visit doesn't make sense? Ed's reading, also to confirm or correct: the
first visit has already delivered what was promised — the conversation, and the rough
or precise read described in Section 5 — so there's no obligation to produce a second
visit, and the attendee leaves with what Section 7 below describes for a
first-visit-only outcome, not with nothing.

## 7. What the attendee leaves with

The script states: *"this is something you can take with you — we'll give you a plan
you can take with you."*

**CONFIRM OR CORRECT — Ed's reading.** The script's sequencing ("second visit... we'll
start building your financial house... this is something you can take with you") is
read here as meaning the written plan is a second-visit-or-later deliverable, not
something produced at a first visit. On this reading, a first visit alone (no second
visit scheduled, per Section 6) does not produce a written plan — the attendee leaves
with the conversation itself and whatever rough or precise read was possible per
Section 5, not a document. If a first visit does in practice produce some written
takeaway even without a second visit — even something short of a full plan — say so
here, since that changes what "take with you" can honestly mean in marketing copy that
doesn't distinguish between visit outcomes.
