## 0. Design decisions up front (so Ed and Justin aren't guessing at my reasoning)

**Same five questions as the card and landing page, kept in sync rather than
independently rewritten.** Two reasons. First, honest one: the questions don't need
to change. A drawdown doesn't change what's in someone's accounts — it changes how
much they want to look. Second, practical one: reusing the card's already-reviewed
Q1-Q5 language (now including the Q2, Q4, and Q5 fixes synced 2026-09-19 — see v2.3
above) keeps this review a diff against known-good copy instead of a fresh read of a
whole new diagnostic. The only genuinely new copy is the framing around the
questions — the open, the bridge line for repeat contacts, the CTA block, and the
close. That's deliberately the smallest new surface I could build this on.

**Channel: email as the primary vehicle. No mail. A narrow, judgment-gated SMS
heads-up, not a blast.**
- *Mail is excluded on timing alone.* Print-and-mail-house turnaround is measured in
  days-to-weeks even on a rush job. The whole point of this piece is that it lands
  while the reader is still looking at their own statement, not two weeks later when
  the moment's cooled. Mail is right for the evergreen base card precisely because it
  isn't in a hurry; it's wrong here.
- *Email is right for the opposite reason: it's fast, and it has room.* This piece
  carries the same disclosure load as the base card (two-hat block, claims-paying-
  ability language) plus new channel-specific requirements (see below) — that doesn't
  fit a text message, and it can go out same-day.
- *SMS: heads-up only, not the vehicle, and only where consent already covers it.*
  I'd use a short text — "Sent you something worth two minutes, check your email" —
  same day as the email, but only to the slice of the list where Justin's office
  already has a documented basis to text (e.g., people who've texted with the office
  before). I would NOT text the whole warm list cold. A phone number collected on a
  class sign-in sheet or an FHA intake form is not automatically consent to receive a
  text — that's a TCPA question, and I'm flagging it rather than assuming it's fine.
  **This needs Justin/Ed to confirm consent basis segment by segment before any text
  goes out; the email can proceed without that question being settled.**

**New compliance surface this piece introduces that the base card never had to deal
with — flagging explicitly so it doesn't get missed under the "already reviewed once"
assumption:**
1. CAN-SPAM (email, not covered by SEC/insurance ad review): needs a working unsubscribe
   mechanism and Trulip's physical address in the footer.
2. TCPA (if any SMS is used): consent basis per segment, "reply STOP" language.
3. Outbound-call recordkeeping (Rule 204-2): every "send it back" contact — who texted
   in, when, what was said on the callback — needs to be logged like any other
   correspondence. This is a new operational habit, not just a copy fix.
4. The response-time promise in the CTA ("we'll call you within two business days") is
   a printed representation, same category as the base card's "no pitch" promise — it
   only survives review if it's operationally true. Don't let this go out as copy
   before it's confirmed as a process.

## Operational trigger rule — what "correction" means, executed without deliberation

The whole point of this piece is that it's ready before a bad week, not written during
one. Here's the rule as I'd hand it to Justin to execute without a strategy meeting:

**1. Benchmark: the S&P 500, closing price index.** One number, the one every retiree
already half-watches on the news. Not a custom composite, not intraday — closing
values only, so a single volatile afternoon that recovers by the bell doesn't fire a
send that then looks premature or panicky the next morning.

**2. Threshold: 15% drawdown from the most recent closing high** (trailing 52-week
high is the practical version of "most recent" — simple, checkable in under a minute
on any finance site or a standing brokerage/Google Finance alert). 15%, not 20% —
waiting for a full bear market misses the window; the referral spike and the anxiety
both start well before "bear market" becomes an official word for it. This is
"call it 15-20%" resolved to the low end on purpose: readiness beats precision here.

**3. One send per episode, not one send per bad day.** Once the 15% trigger fires and
this goes out, it doesn't fire again for the same drawdown even if the market keeps
sliding. A new send is only warranted after the index first recovers back to within 5%
of its prior high (the episode is over) and then drops 15%+ again from a new high.
This is the guardrail against sending three panicky-feeling emails in one bad month.

**4. Who decides: Justin, alone, same day or next business day.** The market-close
check can be automated (a standing price alert costs nothing to set up). The send
decision does not get automated or delegated to a committee — it's a single person's
call because it's also a commitment of his time (the callback SLA below), and only he
knows his week's real bandwidth. Isla or I can flag "the trigger crossed" as an
observation; we don't pull it.

**5. Speed: within 48 hours of the trigger firing.** Content and list are pre-built and
pre-cleared now, not drafted in the moment — that's what this document is for. The only
live decision in a bad week is a one-line gut check: *can the office actually honor a
two-business-day callback window starting this week?* If genuinely no (Justin's own
book is on fire that week too — which, worth naming, is exactly when this trigger
fires), the honest move is to widen the promised window in the copy before sending, not
to send the same promise and quietly miss it. Missing a stated callback window during
the exact week someone was scared enough to reach out is worse than not sending at all.

**One-line version Justin can actually use in a bad week:** *"S&P closed down 15%+ from
its recent high, and it's not a repeat of a send I already made this cycle — send
within 48 hours, after confirming I can honor the two-day callback window."*
