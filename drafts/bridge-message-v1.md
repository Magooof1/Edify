Drafted by Sloan, 2026-09-18. Status: draft for Ed's compliance review, not cleared,
not approved by Justin. Read against CLAUDE.md (top section, corrected 2026-09-18),
`.claude/memory/brand-voice.md`, `drafts/taleb-blue-targeting.md`, and the Taleb/

**Scope check, stated plainly up front:** per Taleb's memo, the Meta advertiser
verification and Calendly build aren't done — nothing here is spendable yet regardless
of how this copy lands with Ed and Justin. This is the asset, not the launch.

**What Blue is, correctly, in this draft:** advisory — Portfolio Medics' active
management, staying invested with a plan for the downside. Not an insurance product.
None of the five versions below describe Blue in insurance terms (no "protected," no
"guaranteed," no annuity-adjacent language) and none touch Green at all — no
principal-protection language, no "can't lose value," nothing that reads as a
description of an annuity or fixed product. That's deliberate, per the brief: Green
pulls in insurance-ad and OH/KY geofence exposure this piece doesn't need, and Blue
alone is enough to do the job.

**Geography note:** none of these versions assume or exclude a specific state. That's
intentional — Taleb's OH/KY-vs-national bet is a live, ungated strategic question
above this piece's pay grade. This copy doesn't take a side on it either way, so it
stays compatible with whichever way that gets resolved.

## Version 1 — "The Sequence Question"

**Headline:** What happens to your income plan if the market drops the year you retire?

**Primary text:**
> Not a crash. Just an ordinary bad stretch, at an inconvenient time.
>
> Growth investing and retirement income are two different problems — one is about
> the money getting bigger, the other is about what happens if it doesn't, right when
> you need to start drawing from it.
>
> There's a way to stay invested where the downside has an actual plan attached to
> it, not just hope that the timing works out.
>
> Five short questions can show you where your own approach stands. Two minutes, no
> pitch.

**CTA:** See where you stand →

**What it's trying to do:** Take the specific, rational version of the Red fear —
sequence-of-returns risk, described in plain language, no jargon — and hand the
reader the existence of an answer in the same breath, without naming it. Closest of
the five to Taleb's "describe the binary the reader already lives inside" argument,
minus the Green-adjacent half of his binary.

**Who it's built to catch:** Someone with real invested assets, close enough to
drawing on them that "the year you retire" isn't abstract, who has had this exact
worry cross their mind even if they've never had a name for it.

**Who it's built to bore:** Anyone years from retirement, anyone with no meaningful
market exposure, anyone who already has this specific gap covered and knows it.

**What could go wrong:** "Growth investing and retirement income are two different
problems" is a definitional claim — educational, not a population claim, but it's
doing real conceptual work in one sentence for a reader who's skimming. If it doesn't
land in the half-second before someone's thumb moves, the whole bridge collapses
before the payoff line. Ed should also double check "two different problems" doesn't
read as implying every other approach treats them as one (a competitor claim in
miniature) — I don't think it does, but it's adjacent enough to be worth his eyes.

## Version 2 — "The Untouched Statement"

**Headline:** The retirement statement you almost didn't open

**Primary text:**
> You know the feeling — a rough week in the market, and for a second you don't want
> to check.
>
> Fair question to sit with: is that because the money is fully exposed, with
> nothing standing between it and whatever happens next? Or because there's already a
> plan for that?
>
> There's a way to invest where the downside has an actual plan built in — not just
> hope that it works out.
>
> If the first question is the one that rings true, five short questions can show
> you where things actually stand. Two minutes, no pitch.

**CTA:** See where you stand →

**What it's trying to do:** Open on the felt, sensory version of the fear instead of
the rational one — the flinch, not the math — then hand the reader a question to
answer for themselves rather than telling them what their flinch means. That
self-answered-question structure is the fix for the earlier draft of this line, which
told the reader their money was exposed; this version makes the reader say it, if
it's true.

**Who it's built to catch:** People who already watch their own statements closely
enough to have felt this specific flinch — self-directed, anxious, engaged investors.

**Who it's built to bore:** Anyone who doesn't personally track their accounts.

**What could go wrong:** Two things. First, tone — this is the most emotionally
forward of the five, closest to the line Ed will scrutinize hardest for
fear-mongering even though it avoids diagnosing the reader directly; it's a
defensible read but not a free one. Second, and I think more important strategically:
this version structurally only catches people who watch their own statements. Per
growth-channels.md, the population Justin's team has already identified as bigger and
underdeveloped — delegators, people who deliberately don't watch their statements
("don't want to know how the steak is made") — will never feel this flinch and won't
recognize themselves here at all. This is the version most likely to just re-catch
people Justin's existing channels already reach.

## Version 3 — Taleb's binary, run straight (I would not ship this one as written)

**Headline:** Retirement money isn't always one thing.

**Primary text:**
> Some retirement money sits safe and earns almost nothing. Some is fully invested
> and fully exposed to whatever the market does next. There's a third way to hold it
> — invested, with an actual plan for the downside built in.
>
> If you've never heard that third option described before, that's worth two
> minutes.
>
> Five short questions will show you where your own money falls. No pitch.

**CTA:** See where you stand →

**What it's trying to do:** This is Taleb's proposed line from `taleb-blue-targeting.md`,
run close to verbatim (with the "nobody has shown you" comparative claim he himself
flagged already removed). It's the theoretically strongest bridge of the five on his
own logic — it names the exact felt binary instead of gesturing at an unnamed third
option.

**Who it's built to catch / bore:** Same profile as Version 1, in theory — but see
below.

**What could go wrong — and why I'm not recommending it run as drafted:** Taleb wrote
this line analyzing message strategy in general. This brief carries a constraint his
memo wasn't testing against: stay entirely off Green. "Sits safe and earns almost
nothing" is a description of Green's shape — principal-protected, low-yield — even
without using the words "guaranteed" or "annuity." That's exactly the feature
description the brief says triggers insurance-ad rules and the OH/KY geofence, and
it's also unnecessary: Blue is the conversion mechanism on its own, per the brief's
own reasoning, so there's no upside to including the Green leg to justify the
compliance exposure. I'm including this version so Justin can see the line Taleb
actually proposed and judge for himself, not because I think it should run as-is.
**Version 1 is functionally the Green-avoidance rewrite of this exact idea** — same
binary logic, minus the half that isn't allowed in this piece.

## Version 4 — "The one question I ask" (Justin's own voice)

**Headline:** The one question I ask everyone who feels behind

**Primary text:**
> I've spent years teaching people how their retirement money is actually positioned
> — not projected, positioned: where it sits right now, and what happens to it if the
> next few years get rough.
>
> One gap I see again and again isn't a lack of savings. It's money that's fully
> invested with no plan for what happens if the market turns at the wrong time — the
> years right before or right after retirement, when there's no time left to just
> wait it out.
>
> If that's ringing a bell, I built a two-minute way to check your own situation.
> Five questions, no pitch.
>
> — Justin

**CTA:** See where you stand →

**What it's trying to do:** Put Justin himself in the guide seat from the first
word, in first person — the most direct StoryBrand execution of the five, and the
version that most resembles his own classroom voice, which is useful if this
eventually feeds the same brand voice as the YouTube live-builds. Uses the carve-out
the brief explicitly allows: Justin describing his own experience, not a population
claim.

**Who it's built to catch:** People who respond to a named, personal guide over an
anonymous brand voice — plausibly a slightly older, more relationship-oriented
reader than the other four, which fits the target state of mind well.

**Who it's built to bore:** People who distrust first-person "I've helped people
like you" framing on sight — a real segment on Facebook.

**What could go wrong:** This is the version I'd send to Ed with the most specific
flag attached. "One gap I see again and again" is closer to the line than the others
— it's Justin's own experience, which the brief permits, but "again and again"
quantifies that experience in a way that edges toward an implied track-record or
prevalence claim, which is the same shape of claim Ed's standing rule exists to
catch, even coming from Justin's own mouth instead of a "most people" construction.
I don't think this crosses the line — it's phrased as personal observation, not
statistic — but it's the one version where I'd want Ed's actual read before assuming
the carve-out covers it, rather than assuming it myself.

## Version 5 — "Not a pitch" (anti-pitch / permission-forward)

**Headline:** This isn't a pitch. It's five questions.

**Primary text:**
> If your instinct is to scroll past anything that looks like a retirement ad, fair
> enough. This is genuinely five questions, nothing else.
>
> They're about how your own retirement money is actually positioned right now — not
> a projection, not a pitch. Fully exposed to the market, or something with an actual
> plan for the downside built in. Two minutes, no calls, no email required, nothing
> to buy.
>
> If you're close enough to retirement for that to matter, it's worth answering
> honestly.

**CTA:** See where you stand →

**What it's trying to do:** Address the reader's ad-fatigue directly instead of
working around it — the most Godin, permission-marketing-literal of the five: it
names the interruption problem and opts out of it in the first line, rather than just
trying not to feel like an ad. "Close enough to retirement for that to matter" does
the state-of-mind filtering job explicitly, without naming an age or dollar figure.

**Who it's built to catch:** Ad-literate, skeptical near-retirees — plausibly
overlapping with the "been to four seminars and never heard this" persona from
growth-channels.md, people who've been pitched before and tuned it out.

**Who it's built to bore:** Anyone who responds normally to benefit-forward ad copy
and doesn't carry active ad-fatigue; anyone not retirement-proximate.

**What could go wrong:** "This isn't a pitch" is itself a well-worn ad trope — a
sharp reader may clock the move for what it is and bounce anyway, which would
undercut exactly the trust it's trying to build. There's also a smaller wording risk:
"nothing to buy" implies a product was ever on the table, when the actual offer is an
advisory relationship, not a sale — Ed may want that line softened or cut so it
doesn't invite a comparison that wasn't there to begin with.

## Ranking

**Run first: Version 1.** Cleanest compliance profile of the five (no Green, no
first-person quantification question, no fear-forward tone), and it does the actual
job Taleb specified — describes the binary the Red-checker is already living inside
— without the constraint violation Version 3 carries. If I only got to run one thing
this week, this is it.

**Run second: Version 4.** Best long-term brand fit — it's Justin's own voice doing
the bridging, which is the version most consistent with what the class already does
and what YouTube will eventually do, so a win here compounds into other channels. Get
Ed's specific read on the quantification question first.

**Worth testing, lower confidence: Version 5.** Genuinely different mechanism (names
the ad-fatigue instead of avoiding it) rather than a rewrite of the same idea — if it
works, it may reach a segment the others miss entirely. Real chance it reads as
gimmicky instead of trustworthy; that's a live risk, not a hunch I've resolved.

**Fourth: Version 2.** Strong emotionally, but I'd want to be honest with Justin
about what it's actually testing: it re-catches statement-watchers, which is
close to the population his existing channels already reach, not the delegator
population the team has already flagged as bigger and untapped. Fine to run, not
where I'd put the first dollar.

**Kill as written: Version 3.** Not because the underlying idea is weak — it's
Taleb's own proposed line and the theoretically tightest bridge of the five — but
because it violates this brief's own Green-avoidance constraint. Version 1 is the
fix; there's no reason to run the unfixed version alongside it.

## Where I think the whole approach is weak

Three things, and I don't think any of them are Taleb's already-made points restated.

**First: the ad is being asked to do more trust-building than the brief credits it
for.** The brief frames the ad's job as narrow — "make the Red-feeling person click,
it doesn't have to deliver Blue." I don't think that's quite right. The eval-form
conversion happens after 40 minutes in a room with Justin, where he's already earned
some credibility, answered objections live, and the reader has nowhere else to look.
A cold ad has none of that — a stranger has to make a category-level leap ("I have a
specific unmet need, and this ad, not the fifty others in my feed, is the one telling
me the truth about it") off of roughly 60 words with zero relationship behind them.
That's a bigger ask than "get the click." I think the honest read is that these
versions aren't just filtering for a state of mind — they're also trying to do a
compressed version of the trust Justin builds live, in the same breath as the
filtering, and that's a genuinely hard needle to thread in ad copy, not a solved
problem because the copy avoids jargon. Worth watching for in the results: if CTR is
fine but click-to-mirror engagement is weak, that's this problem showing up, not a
targeting problem.

**Second: none of these, even the best one, reach the delegator population.** Every
version here — even the anti-pitch one — depends on the reader having *some* active
relationship with their own account: watching it, worrying about it, feeling a
flinch. Growth-channels.md already flags that the people Justin's team thinks are
bigger and wealthier are the ones who deliberately don't do that. This ad campaign,
run perfectly, is a good test of "can we reach people who'd have come to the class
anyway, without the class" — which is a real and useful thing to know — but it is
not a delegator solve, and I'd tell Justin that plainly rather than let the framing
("this piece is the thing that puts people in front of him") imply it's solving both
problems at once. It's solving one.

**Third, and this is the blunt one: this can't be the thing that puts 25 people in
front of Justin this week, and I don't think it's honest to write it as though it
could be.** Per Taleb's own note, Meta advertiser verification and the Calendly build
aren't done. Even assuming Ed clears one of these tomorrow and Justin signs off same
day, there's real calendar time — weeks, not days — before a single click happens.
Justin's been blocked on regulatory mechanics for a week; good copy sitting behind
unbuilt plumbing doesn't unblock that clock, it just means the copy is ready when the
plumbing is. I'd rather say that now than have this land on his desk framed as the
fix and have the gap between "drafted" and "running" read as another delay.

That's not an argument against the bridge framing itself — I think Taleb's core
insight (Red-checkers are anxious about exactly what Blue answers, so bridge the
specific worry to the specific answer) is right, and Version 1 is built to prove or
disprove it cleanly. It's an argument against treating this piece as solved once it's
written. It's a test of a mechanism, with a real chance it needs a second round once
real people see it — not a lead machine on the first shot.
