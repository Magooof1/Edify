## 6. What I'd test, and what the test cannot read

**January cannot choose between these.** The record gives roughly 10-20 households per $10,000 in the good
years and 3 on the last drop. A 5,000-piece drop split across two fronts yields single-digit registrations
per arm, which is noise at any plausible rate. I will not recommend a split.

**The one test that can run before print: a three-second test on A, six to eight people in the 55-65 band
who are not clients.** Show Option 1's A and Option 2's A for three seconds each, order alternated, plus
two baselines (the control's mail panel and the v6 "Find out if anything's missing" front). Ask:
1. *What is this?* (open answer, before any prompt)
2. *Who is it from, and is it free or paid?*
3. *Would you open it?*
4. *What did you think it was selling?*

**What it can read:** failures. Do they know it is a class? Do they think it is an ad from a financial
adviser? Do they get "retirement" in three seconds? **What it cannot read:** which front pulls more
registrations. Eight people cannot rank two pieces; do not let it be reported as a ranking.

**My fallback rule, set before I see any result so it is not a post-hoc rationalisation:** if three or
more of eight read Option 2's A as a financial adviser's advertisement and not a class, I recommend
Option 1. That threshold is my judgment, not an evidence-based number.

**Headline set, for the same test (taste is not evidence):**
1. "Do I have enough?"
2. "How would I even know if I have enough?"
3. "Do I have enough? What about health insurance?"
4. "What happens to my income if the market drops the year I retire?"
5. *(baseline)* the control's "New academic courses in a fun, interactive learning environment..."
6. *(baseline)* v6's "Find out if anything's missing from your retirement."

**Also, before anything: photograph the pieces in Justin's mailbox** (section 1, push back 2).

**An instrument the drop can carry.** Give B and C **different URL paths and QR codes** (same vanity
