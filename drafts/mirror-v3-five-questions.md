## 1. Verdicts on the three points

### Point 1 — "They interrogate one room of a four-room house." PARTLY AGREE.

**Agree on the mismatch, and it is worse than you stated.** The cover promises "find out if
anything's missing from your retirement." v6's panel L then names the house as "your money,
your income, what you leave behind, your health and care." The mirror delivers the first
word of four. Under the cover check's second half, the reader cannot tell the instrument
matches the promise.

**Two things you did not say that make it worse:**

- **The old mirror teaches the taxonomy cold.** Its key is three bucket descriptions. The
  altitude rule (`brand-voice.md`) says a cold piece sells the feeling and the class delivers
  the specific thing, and that "you're missing a bucket" is a taxonomy claim about something
  a cold reader has never heard of. The money mirror is a mid-class instrument (buckets
  arrive about an hour in) mounted on a cold piece. That is the "wrong end of the class"
  failure again, in the instrument rather than the headline.
- **Its vocabulary already diverges from the class's own.** The README on the Step 2 Form
  logs it: the class says Principal Protected / Manage Risk / Accept Risk; the mirror says
  "can't lose value" / "managed with a plan" / "moving on its own." The continuity problem
  exists before anyone argues about rooms.

**Disagree on "one room of four" as if the rooms were equal.** Money is the foundation: the
class starts there (script slide 4: "the foundation is what we're going to start with"), and
Justin's own account is that "if someone's going to work with me, they know it at this
point," roughly slide 59, the end of the money section. The last ~90 minutes (income,
legacy, roof) "do no further conversion work" (`class-content.md`, 2026-09-22). So the
money-only mirror is not aimed at the wrong room. It is aimed at the converting room and
**sold as the whole house.** That is a labelling defect with a cheap fix (section 3), not
necessarily a content defect. It is also why my house draft gives money two of the five.

**The strongest reason for the house is a different one, and it is about the outside of the
piece.** The mirror sits on inside-right, the third surface a reader reaches. The 3-second
decision is made on the cover and the mailing panel. What the mailing panel carries about
the mirror is the qualifier bullets ("This class is for you if...") and the band "Inside:
five questions." Those lines are the outward face of the instrument. The house questions
make better outside copy than the money ones, because they are felt gaps in the reader's
own voice ("you couldn't say what you'd have coming in each month") rather than a product
description ("you have money in savings or CDs"). That is reasoning, not data.

### Point 2 — "Q5 breaks the promise printed above it." AGREE ON THE TENSION, DISAGREE ON WHAT IT FORCES.

**Agree:** nobody weighed the promise. The v2.3 note (`mirror-card-v2.md`) weighed length
(+44%), the question count, and the three-bucket frame. It never mentions the intro line
"you don't need to know anything about investing." You are right that nobody chose.

**Already on record, and it sharpens the point:** `class-content.md`, 2026-09-22
CORRECTION. Justin's real class question ("Would you classify that as red or blue?") "only
works because the room already has a taxonomy to classify against — a cold reader has none,
which is exactly why the invented substitute had to build one from scratch and was
structurally weaker." The disqualifying sentence in Q5 *is* that substitute, compressed into
a clause. So the tension was predictable from the record, and the record also says why it
cannot be solved by wording.

**Where I disagree: you framed it as teach-or-keep-the-promise. That is not the real
choice.** The choice is between two kinds of key:

- **Classifier key** (the current one). The key tells the reader what their money *is*
  ("Money that's managed with a plan for the downside"). To be accurate, every question
  feeding it has to be accurate, so Q2 needed "same," Q4 needed "that old account," the OR
  became an AND, and Q5 needed a lecture. Each patch was reasonable. Together they are a
  sign the design is trying to do something a tick-box cannot.
- **Report key.** The key repeats what the reader said ("Money where I could say what
  limits a bad year"). No question has to be a perfect classifier, because the key claims
  nothing beyond the tick. A 60/40 reader who ticks yes is *accurately reported* as someone
  who could say what limits a bad year. Whether they are right is the class's job, and it
  is the room's 60/40 slide that does it ("would you classify that as red or blue?").

**One observation on the scoring logic, offered as a reading of the text, not a finding.**
After the "that same money" and "that old account" anchors, Q2's yes implies Q1's yes, and
Q4's yes implies Q3's yes. Read literally, "Q1 or Q2" collapses to Q1 and "Q3 and Q4" to
Q4. The only readers the extra questions change are careless ones, and for them Q2 can only
add a false credit (a yes on Q2 from a reader who said no to Q1, thinking of brokerage
cash). I do not know Q2's design intent and I have not asked Sloan, so treat this as
inferred from the wording. If it holds, the key's logic is vestigial.

**So my choice on point 2:** keep the promise ("no investing knowledge"), cut the
disqualifying sentence, and stop trying to catch the confident-and-wrong reader inside the
instrument. No self-check can correct someone who is sure it is handled, and the product's
actual correction ("something you were sure was handled isn't what you thought") happens in
the room, with a taxonomy installed first. **The mirror's job on a cold piece is to produce
doubt in the reader who is already uncertain**, which is the altitude rule again. Both of my
drafts do this. The house just does it with a cleaner key.

### Point 3 — "The scoring asks the reader to do boolean logic." AGREE, SMALLER THAN STATED.

Three rules over five questions, an OR and an AND, is more than a two-minute pencil card
should ask. But per the Q2 observation above, a careful reader barely needs the logic, and
**nobody has watched a reader score this card.** Sloan's v1 flag is still open in
`mirror-card-v2.md`: "the mapping step should be tested on five real people with a printed
mockup before a mail house sees it." It was never run. I rate this a real but second-order
defect. The house key (pure transcription, one box per question) removes it entirely, which
is a point in the house's favour, not the reason to choose it.

### 2c. Ed's prohibition on undeniable verdicts: can a rooms key be built inside the line?

**Yes. It does not inherently cross it.** The FB "Financial House Snapshot" failed because
it printed adjectives ("Protection: Strong," "Taxes: Moderate") that were *not derived from
the answers*. A key that prints only what the reader ticked is the opposite. Four build
rules keep it there:

1. **One box per question, transcription only.** No combining logic, so the key cannot
   generate a conclusion the reader did not write.
2. **Labels are the class's neutral part names and the reader's own words.** No adjective
   about the reader's situation, ever.
3. **The only interpretive sentence is mechanical:** "An empty box means you ticked 'I'd
   have to find out.' That's all it means." It says nothing about the reader's money.
4. **No tally, no "3 of 5," no color, no icon on an empty box,** and nothing that makes a
   blank look like damage. A designer pairing visuals can introduce an implication without
   anyone reviewing it as copy (`brand-assets/README.md`, the whiteboard caveat), so the
   graphic goes to Ed with the copy.

**Residual risks, stated so Ed does not have to find them:**

- **Validity.** The instrument measures *recall*, not *coverage*. A reader whose beneficiaries
  are perfectly set but who cannot recall the names gets an empty box. That is true to what
  the page says ("from memory") and consistent with the product ("the relief of finding
  out"), but it will produce blanks in people who are fine. The weakest honest reading of
  the key: "here is what I could say today," not "here is what is missing." The cover line
  ("find out if anything's missing") is delivered by the class, not by this panel, and the
  panel's own heading is worded to match what it measures.
- **The frame is a claim.** Printing four parts implies a retirement has four parts. That is
  the class's educational frame, general and not about the reader, but Ed should look at it.
- **Q1, Q4 and Q5 describe no product.** Nothing here describes principal protection, an
  annuity, an insurance policy or an advisory service. Whether "care at home for a year"
  edges toward describing long-term care insurance is Ed's call. I think it does not.

### 2d. Q-by-Q audit against the card's own test ("can a reader not in that state get credited?")

own tick. The failure class that remains is the confident-and-wrong reader, and the
