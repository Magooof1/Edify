## The three questions

### 1. The instructor rules, and the hypothesis test

You asked me to test "the rule that closes these venues to most advisors is the rule that opens them to Justin, because no product, no pitch is already his positioning."

**Verdict: half right, and the missing half is the conversion mechanism.**

What holds:
- **The kill condition is not met.** You said that if these venues exclude anyone licensed regardless of content, the category is dead. I found no such policy at any of them, and CHPL states the opposite in writing. Content that is general-interest and requires no purchase is permitted from a business representative there.
- **The OLLI network norm is compatible with education content.** Other OLLIs' published rules (San Diego and UConn, not UC) say there is "no promotion, selling or solicitation of business" during classes, allow the presenter to state an affiliation to establish credentials, and allow business cards after class. That is the shape of Justin's class content.

What fails:
- **The venue rule is about the room, and "no pitch" as Ed drafted it is about the first visit.** `drafts/no-pitch-policy-note.md` states policy for the Step 2 visit: no product, no application, no signature. It says nothing about what happens in the class. The class's own last act is the Step 2 Form: "Next Step of the class is to schedule to discuss your areas of concern," a printed grid of eight times, "Circle your top 2," confirmation within 48 hours (`reference/class/README.md`). To a library or OLLI reviewer, a hand-out that books the presenter's business a meeting is the definition of soliciting business. CHPL's permitted alternative is a take-home brochure that cannot be "distributed as part of the event."
- **The content itself could be read either way (E).** The class ends on the discovery that a middle bucket exists and the attendee does not have it (`growth-channels.md`, 2026-09-12). The record (`growth-channels.md`, 2026-09-23, a paraphrase of his position, not his verbatim) says the plan's conclusion "points at something they can't deliver," meaning something he does. A reviewer deciding whether content is "applicable to situations that do not require purchasing a specific product" could read that as education or as a setup. I cannot settle that from here; it is the reviewer's call and Ed's exposure.
- **"No pitch" cannot be a moat, because it is the universal claim.** Retirement Planning University's listing says specific financial products will not be discussed (D). Every advisor who gets in says the same. The gate filters behavior, not positioning, and this institution has heard the positioning before.

**The UC OLLI instructor rules themselves: unverified.** A document titled "OLLI Curriculum Committee Standing Rules" exists as a public PDF at `uc.edu/content/dam/refresh/cont-ed-62/olli/cc-rules.pdf` (B, its existence; contents unread, fetch blocked). That is a public document, not a contact. Reading it is the cheapest way to replace my inference with the actual text.

### 2. Who owns the audience

Nothing I found lets Justin capture a registration list, an email, or a follow-up.

- **UC OLLI:** the registrant is the University's member. Other OLLIs' published policies say member information is not shared with outside groups (American University) or that rosters go to course leaders only for attendance and course communication (Minnesota) (C). UC's own policy on rosters: **unverified.** A mailer and an e-newsletter exist for OLLI members (B), and both belong to OLLI.
- **Libraries:** Ohio Revised Code 149.432 restricts disclosure of library patron information; Kentucky protection is reported to rest on Attorney General opinions (C). That a program registration list would count as patron information is my inference (E). The only take-home capture CHPL's language allows is a card the attendee chooses to pick up.
- **Continuing-ed catalogs (NKU, Xavier, others):** unverified.

**This is the asymmetry that decides the comparison.** With mail, every registrant is Justin's response, tagged, followed up. With a hosted audience, he owns only what self-selects to pick up a card. And the funnel he has is 90% in-room booking. So the real question about this category is not "does it fill a room" but "does a room with no in-room booking step convert at all." **Nothing on record measures that.** The ~90% is a property of class-shaped attendees who were handed the form (per `offer-options-and-ranking.md`); it should not be carried into this category as an assumption.

### 3. Room size and intent, said plainly

**High attendance, low intent. That is the risk.** OLLI members enrol out of interest, not out of a problem they want solved. They pay $90 a term to learn things, most of them from volunteers, on any topic in the catalog. A retirement-finance talk is one item in a 100-to-200-course menu. Libraries are similar: the audience is the calendar's audience, drawn by the topic and the free room. Neither audience has spent a Saturday morning on this. A cold-mailed class attendee did, and that sacrifice is part of why the class converts (with the caveat Taleb put on it: the sacrifice cannot explain the 90%, but it plausibly explains who is in the room).

I could not verify room size for any candidate, so I am not quoting a body count. The 20-30 seats in the brief is unverified, and I will not lend it a number.

**Metric: cost per client, not cost per body** (Jayme's correction). Arithmetic on one stated base, illustrative only:

- Base from the record: the class is about 4.5 Justin-hours per session and about 1.3 hours per household at today's turnout (`offer-options-and-ranking.md`).
- **My assumption, stated once: a one-time presentation costs about 3 Justin-hours** (prep of the compressed cut, travel, a 60-to-75-minute talk). Not a sourced figure.
- On that base, matching the class's 1.3 hours per household takes **about 2 to 3 booked households per talk.** If a room is 15 people, that is roughly 13-20% of the room booking through a take-home card, against a class where the form is handed to people already in the room and ~90% of attendees book. I know of no basis for a card doing that.
- The cash cost is $0, against roughly $3,333 per unit on the April 2026 mailer and a planned $1,800-2,500. So on cash it wins at any yield. **The constraint that matters is Justin's hours, which is Stage B's constraint in `ROADMAP.md`, not the mail budget.**
- Also: **this category gives the $7K perk nothing to fund.** A free library or OLLI room has no rental to reimburse, so it does not solve the perk's "needs a home" problem. (E, from the perk rules on record.)

## Ranking and recommendation

**My read: this category is weaker than the credit-union route already on record, as a replacement for the NKU funnel.** I would rather say that than dress it up.

Why, stated on structure since the proof is thin on both sides:

- **The gatekeeper problem is identical.** Justin's standing objection ("it requires to get permission from them") applies to a Curriculum Committee, a library program coordinator, and a continuing-ed office exactly as it applies to a credit union. This category does not dissolve it. Gated channels are more durable if you clear them; this one is no exception and no better.
- **The audience fit is worse.** Credit-union members are working households, plausibly nearer 54-64. OLLI members skew retired and 65+ (national survey, C). Library audiences are broad and unqualified.
- **The one thing the category adds is real:** genuine institutional co-hosting rather than simulated. But the institution keeps the audience, the follow-up, and the rules of the room, and the follow-up is where this practice's economics live.
- **The caveat, so this is not read as overconfident:** the credit-union route is *unverified* on its own key question (does a standing calendar exist), and this category is only *partly* verified, mostly B-grade. I am ranking on structure, not on proof, and both should be settled with the public reads above before either drives a decision.

**Ranking, in order of what I would do:**

1. **Ask Justin how NKU is booked (question 1 above).** Zero cost, and it is the highest-value fact here. It decides whether "already in the building" exists at NKU Community Connections, and it settles the Ed question about the NKU framing. Not a channel yet, a question that comes before any channel.
2. **Public libraries, as a bounded test, only after Justin approves contact.** One library-sponsored talk, most likely CHPL first because it is the only system with an explicit written allowance for business presenters, with Kenton and Campbell behind it on the Kentucky side. Frame it as a test of a single question: **does a room with no in-room booking step produce any inbound at all?** Design it so it can answer: passive take-home card only, unique URL and QR and a source tag (the same four non-negotiables the mail carries, from `growth-channels.md`, Lever 3). Ed pre-clears the compressed deck and the card before anything is proposed. Small bounded downside: a few of Justin's hours; no cash; no list dependence.
3. **UC OLLI, one one-time presentation, only after someone reads the Standing Rules PDF and Justin decides he wants a retiree-skewed room.** Real standing catalog, real audience, but the wrong end of the age range and the lowest intent. Not a volume play. Target Winter or Spring 2027 at the earliest.
4. **Drop for now:** Miami ILR (Oxford, outside radius); Xavier (rental, and Retirement Planning University is already there); Thomas More, Gateway CTC, Cincinnati State, Great Oaks (nothing found that fits); school-district adult ed (nothing found; unverified).

**What I would not do:** pitch this to Justin as a way to fill NKU's empty chairs. It is not that. It is a credibility tool and a small-room experiment, and its central unknown, whether the class's conversion survives without the in-room form, is exactly the thing this workforce has no data on and the thing the ~90% figure cannot answer.

**What would change my mind:** the public reads show UC OLLI or a library expressly permits a presenter's follow-up offer (a sign-up, a sign-in with consent, a post-talk sign-up sheet); or Justin says NKU Community Connections runs a catalog he can list in.
