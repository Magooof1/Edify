- **Justin McGuffey** is an investment adviser representative (IAR) of **Portfolio

  Retirement Planning" trade name.**

brand name (Trulip Planning, LLC) is not the entity doing the transacting either.
Every variant below is built around keeping those two facts straight rather than
collapsing them for the sake of a shorter sentence.

## 2. Ed's answer to the question Justin routed this for

**Is "Trulip Retirement Planning, a d/b/a of Trulip Planning, LLC" a safe fix?** No.
Justin's read is correct, with one refinement on *which* rule it trips:

- It is **not primarily a Rule 206(4)-1 problem in itself** — the Marketing Rule
  governs Portfolio Medics' advisory marketing, and Trulip Planning/Trulip Investment
  Management aren't RIAs or subject to that rule directly for their insurance business.
- It **is** an **Ohio/Kentucky insurance-advertising and producer-identification
  problem**: both states' insurance advertising frameworks turn on the ad accurately
  identifying the producer/agency actually transacting the business with the consumer.
  Naming Trulip Planning, LLC — an upline FMO that collects overrides and has no
  contractual relationship with the client — as the entity "offering" insurance
  products to that client misidentifies the transacting party. That is a misstatement
  of fact about who the consumer is doing business with, not a simplification.
- It becomes **a Rule 206(4)-1 problem indirectly** on any piece that also carries
  Portfolio Medics' advisory disclosure in the same block: an inaccurate insurance-side
  sentence sitting next to the advisory disclosure undermines the "not misleading, fair
  and balanced" standard for the disclosure as a whole, since a reader has no way to
  know one sentence is checked and the other isn't.

**Bottom line:** the "offered through" / "conducts business through" sentence for
insurance must name **Trulip Investment Management, LLC**, not Trulip Planning, LLC and
not the bare DBA. See Section 3 for how the brand name still gets used without
reintroducing the problem.

**Flagging one thing Justin didn't ask about but the facts raise:** Trulip Investment
Management, LLC receiving **AUM fees from Portfolio Medics** (not just insurance
commissions from carriers) is worth a second look on its own. If that fee is
compensation for referring or soliciting advisory clients to Portfolio Medics, that is
the fact pattern the Marketing Rule's promoter/solicitor provisions (the old Cash
Solicitation Rule, folded into 206(4)-1) are built to catch — separate disclosure and
possibly a written agreement, independent of the insurance-side questions. **This is
outside counsel's question, not resolved here** — flagging so it doesn't get missed
under the assumption that "AUM fees" is just routine revenue-sharing language.

## 8. Ed's answers to Justin's four questions

**Q1 — Should the DBA appear in disclosures at all?**

Position: **no, not in the legal "offered/conducted through" sentence — yes, as a
brand identifier elsewhere.** The DBA is a marketing name; the "offered through" line
is a factual claim about who the client is contracting with, and that claim has to name
Trulip Investment Management, LLC every time, full stop. Folding the DBA into that
sentence (even correctly captioned as "a d/b/a of Trulip Planning, LLC") reintroduces
the exact confusion this rebuild exists to remove, because it puts the FMO's name in
the same sentence as the consumer transaction. Keep the two jobs separate: Variant A/B/C
carry the legal fact; Variant E carries the brand. This is Ed's judgment call, not a
cited rule — a plausible counter-position (state the DBA and its holder transparently,
right next to the real transacting entity, so nothing is hidden) exists and is worth
outside counsel's view if Justin wants a second opinion, but Ed's read is that
transparency about a name that isn't the transacting party still doesn't make it safe
to put in the sentence that identifies the transacting party.

**Q2 — Does Edify need its own disclosure treatment?**

Yes, and the existing line is worth replacing, not just re-pointing. "Edify Retirement
Education is the educational arm of Trulip Retirement Planning" now reads as claiming a
parent that is itself just a trade name — describing a brand as the "arm" of a DBA
doesn't actually answer what legal entity stands behind Edify, it just moves the
ambiguity one level down. Variant A above replaces it with: "Edify Retirement Education
is the brand name under which Justin McGuffey shares general financial and retirement
education. It is not a separate legal entity." This states the true fact (no legal
existence) without implying a corporate relationship to Trulip Planning or Trulip
Investment Management that hasn't been confirmed.

**Q3 — Meta verification, which entity?**

Ed's read: the split Justin describes is plausible and probably right in shape —
**business verification under Trulip Planning, LLC** (it holds the trade name /
marketing brand, and Meta's business verification is fundamentally about who owns the
brand being advertised), **advertiser verification for the financial-services category
under Trulip Investment Management, LLC or Justin's individual OH/KY producer license**
(Meta's financial-services category wants proof of the actual licensed/regulated
capacity conducting the business, which is Trulip Investment Management's role, not
Trulip Planning's). This is Ed's structural read, not a confirmed Meta policy
citation — Meta's own requirements should be confirmed directly (or via Sloan's prior
secondary-source research) before this is treated as settled.

**Flag, separate from the split itself:** the Meta business portfolio is currently
named "Edify Retirement Education." If business verification runs under Trulip
Planning, LLC, that portfolio name will not match the verified legal name — Meta's
verification flow generally wants the two to reconcile (a "doing business as" field, or
a rename). This is worth surfacing now, before verification is attempted a second time,
not after another rejection.

**Q4 — Is "Edify Retirement Education" registered anywhere as a trade name?**

Framed as a question for Justin, not asserted: Justin has said Edify has no documents of
its own. If Edify is actively used in Ohio commerce (letterhead, ads, a Facebook Page,
signage) without an Ohio Secretary of State trade name / fictitious name registration,
that may be worth knowing independent of anything in this file — Ohio law generally
expects a name used in commerce that isn't someone's own legal name to be registered.
Ed is not asserting a violation here, has not checked Ohio's current filing requirement
in detail, and is flagging this as a question worth Justin's attention (or a quick
question to whoever handles Trulip's own entity filings) rather than a finding.
