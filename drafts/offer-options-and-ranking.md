## What the evidence actually supports, and what it doesn't

**Honest summary from the search: nobody has good public data on what drives attendance
to cold-invited free retirement classes for still-working 50–65 households, by length,
day of week, or group size.** Jayme looked and found nothing he would pass on as fact.
He discarded the vendor "best practice" figures (invite 10–14 days out, reminders lift
attendance 15–20%) because seminar-marketing companies sell seminars.

**Two findings that cut against shortening the class:**

- **The 2021 control was a four-hour Saturday class**, 8:30–12:30. **Length was constant
  across the strong years and the weak ones.**
- **Competitors in these ZIPs run formats as long or longer.** Retirement Planning
  University runs a two-night course, 6:00–8:30 each night, at NKU and Xavier; its April
  2026 NKU listing showed a waitlist. Retire Wise Academy runs three hours in
  Cincinnati. (A waitlist is an organiser-set cap, not attendance — but somebody is
  filling classes at NKU right now.)

**The strongest external evidence points at a lever Justin has ruled out.** Duflo & Saez
(*QJE* 2003) found a modest cash reward multiplied attendance at a free university
benefits fair more than fivefold. Bhattacharya et al. (*Review of Financial Studies*
2012) found ~5% take-up of free unbiased advice among ~8,000 brokerage customers, with
those who needed it most least likely to accept. **Value at the door — a meal or money —
is the best-evidenced attendance lever in this domain, and it is off the table by
Justin's own principle.** Everything ranked below instead lowers the reader's cost in
time, friction or exposure.

## The ranked offers

Baseline: the current class costs ~4.5 Justin-hours per session, ~1.3 h per household at
today's turnout, plus a 1-hour Step 2 visit.

### 1. Mirror, then a visit near you — Jayme's recommendation

*Answer five private questions at home in two minutes, then book a 45-minute
conversation with Justin at a public place near you, or a 20-minute call if that's
easier.*

~1.5–2 Justin-hours per body who shows — **a wash against the class once Step 2 is
counted**, so any win has to come from bodies. It changes the genre so the three-second
bin reflex doesn't fire; the first step is private, which matters because information
doesn't cure the approachability barrier; and it reuses the Step 2 structure that
already converts warm at ~90%.

Against it: nothing yet tests the mirror's mechanism, the reader makes two decisions
(score, then book), and meetings from this route will likely close well below 90%.

at the point Justin himself says conversion completes, and it runs Taleb's un-run

Against it: same crowded genre; a weeknight puts it head-to-head with Retirement

### 5. Open office hours, no registration

acute need; a cold-mailed stranger has neither. **And it leaves no response stage to
measure.** "First come" is also scarcity-adjacent and inside Ed's wall.

## ISLA'S PREMISE, CORRECTED BY JAYME

I briefed this as **"maximise bodies in front of Justin per dollar,"** on the grounds
that he converts ~90% of people who show up.

**He's right that this is wrong.** The 90% is a property of *class-shaped* bodies —
people who have already sat through ~45 minutes with no product in it and ~2 hours of
frame-building. Justin's own line, around slide 59: *"if someone's going to work with
me, they know it at this point."* **A stranger walking in cold for a one-to-one has had
none of that. The class produces the 90%, not the hour.**

**Consequence: track cost per client, not cost per body.** Cheap bodies that don't close
would look like success on my metric while failing on the only one that matters.

## AND A CORRECTION TO MY OWN TEST PLAN

`drafts/mirror-mail-test-plan.md` says **under 1 booked meeting per 1,000 pieces means
mail is finished.** Jayme's arithmetic says that rule is too strong.

At 2,500 pieces, **a channel truly delivering 1.5 bookings per 1,000 shows two or fewer
about 28% of the time** — under the line, on a channel that works. And its break-even of
2 per 1,000 assumed ~50% of booked meetings close, taken from Harrison (2 meetings, 1
client, n=2, and class-sourced). At a 25% close rate for cold-booked meetings,
**break-even moves to roughly 4 per 1,000.**

**Two flat cells would say "not a 5x effect." They would not say "mail is finished."**
That distinction needs to be in the plan before anyone reads a result against it.
