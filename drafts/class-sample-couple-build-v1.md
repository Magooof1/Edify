# The sample-couple build — class format design

**Design by Sloan, 2026-09-23. Amendments by Isla, marked as such.**

**Status: DRAFT. Not approved by Justin. NOT reviewed by Ed — Justin explicitly held
this back from compliance for now.** Nothing here is built or scheduled: no slides, no

## Where it came from

Justin's first version (2026-09-23): have attendees bring their own numbers and build
their own plan in the room, reaching the Blue conclusion themselves. Taleb attacked it
on three grounds Isla accepted — attendees reasoning aloud about their own money in
front of strangers fires the approachability barrier that keeps the target segment
away; facilitating 3–15 people at different speeds is a far harder room than a fixed
script; and improvising against real personal numbers multiplies advice-shaped moments
and removes the safety margin a fixed script provides.

**Justin's own revision resolves all three:** build a *fictional* couple's plan live
from the front. His words: *"They are then like that's me."*

## The couple

One fictional household — "the Johnsons" as a placeholder, Justin to rename. Ages
58/60, retiring in 5–7 years, savings in the mid six figures. **Introduced explicitly
as invented:** *"Not a real couple. Not anyone's real numbers — I built this to be
typical of the room, not to match any one of you."*

That line is load-bearing. It is the only thing standing between "sample plan" and
"case study that reads as a testimonial." Ed's exact phrasing required before use.

Accounts chosen to reuse content already in the script rather than invent new material:

| Account | Size | Job in the sequence |
|---|---|---|
| CDs + money market | ~$80k | Easy win. Room gets Green immediately. |
| Individual bonds | ~$40k | First wrinkle. Slots into the existing "why bonds aren't Green" section — purchasing power, 1975–2024, inverse rate relationship. No new content. |
| **Old 401(k), former employer, untouched, target-date / 60-40** | **~$350k** | **The disqualifier.** Same shape as slide 53's Vanguard 60/40 moment, run on the couple instead of an abstract fund. |
| Current employer 401(k), mostly S&P index | ~$150k | Confirming Red. No surprise — cements the lesson right after the disqualifier trips the room. |

### ISLA'S AMENDMENT 1 — the Johnsons are engineered, and the design should say so

Sloan's draft states the result (meaningful Green, meaningful Red, zero Blue) "isn't
engineered — it's what a realistic account list actually sorts to." Both cannot be
fully true. The accounts were chosen. It **is** engineered — engineered to match
reality, which is defensible, but it should be named rather than smoothed over.

It matters because of Justin's constraint (below). **The Johnsons cannot land on "what
you have is fine." They produce the same gap every session, by construction.** Combined
with Justin's own read that essentially nobody arrives holding Blue, the class
demonstrates a gap everyone in the room has.

**So the neutrality is real but it runs on _risk tolerance_, not on bucket mix.** "Red
is fine for you" is the honest landing place. "You already have Blue" never will be.
State that explicitly in the delivery notes, because the moment the narration drifts to
*"see — this is what everybody has,"* it has become the population claim Ed has cut
three times.

## Two participation modes, protecting against different things

**Mode A — the Johnsons. Call-and-response, out loud.** Before each account is
revealed, the room calls out together: *"Green, Blue, or Red — what do you think?"*

This is the mechanism already proven at slide 53 (*"would you classify that as red or
blue?"* — the room answers "red"). It works because it is a **chorus, not a hand
raised or a name called** — nobody is individually exposed even though it is spoken
aloud, because nobody can tell who said what. Reused, not invented.

**Mode B — their own money. Silent, pen only.** After the Johnsons are fully sorted,
the room fills a private worksheet at their seats. Never spoken, never polled, **never
collected.**

**No debrief poll.** Deliberately designed against — "who found they have no Blue?"
would reintroduce the exposure the worksheet exists to remove, and would have Justin
constructing a live, unsubstantiated population claim in front of the room. The no-poll
rule also removes the information Justin would be tempted to nudge on (see risk 2).

---

## The beat sequence

1. **Meet the Johnsons** — invented, stated plainly.
2. **Sort CDs and bonds** (Mode A, chorus) — two easy wins, room builds confidence.
3. **First worksheet pass** — *"now do your own easy ones the same way."* Private.
4. **Sort the old 401(k)** (Mode A, chorus) — room confidently says Blue; it's Red.
   The beat the whole design exists for: same realization as the live-build idea, none
   of the exposure.
5. **Recovery math** (already in the script — lose 20, need 25) lands on the Johnsons'
   account rather than an abstract fund.
6. **Sort the current 401(k)** — confirming Red, no surprise, cements it.
7. **Second worksheet pass** — *"go back to what you marked. Anything in there that
   might be doing what the Johnsons' old 401(k) was doing?"* Highest-value beat in the
   design: hands the disqualifier straight back to the attendee's own paper, privately,
   right after they watched it happen to someone else.
8. **The neutral close, scripted:** *"Whatever you found — more green, more red, a
   little of both, none of blue — that's information, not a verdict. Some of you are
   going to look at that and feel completely fine. Some of you won't. Either one is a
   legitimate place to land."*

Beat 8 turns Justin's stated position into a **written line** so it survives contact
with a live room instead of depending on him remembering to say it.

## Justin's hard constraint, 2026-09-23

> *"I don't care if you have green, blue or red. I do all 3. I won't even steer them
> one way or another. They will just come to their own conclusion based off their risk
> tolerance. Some people are fine with red."*

If the structure can only produce one answer it stops being education and becomes a
funnel with a worksheet, and the practice's entire differentiation dies with it. See
Amendment 1 for where the neutrality actually lives.

## The worksheet artifact

Deliberately bare. Three columns using **Justin's own labels** — Principal Protected /
Manage Risk — Tactical Investments / Accept Risk — Buy and Hold. Blank lines for
account type and round size. No name, no phone, no score, no interpretive text, nothing
that renders a verdict. **It sorts; it does not diagnose** — structurally the opposite
of the mirror's Q5 problem and the FB quiz's "Financial House Snapshot."

**Design rule, not a detail: it must never grow a name, phone or email field.** That
would blur it into the Step 2 form. One artifact is anonymous and leaves with them; the
other is named and gets collected. Keeping them visibly different is the point.

## Placement — reskin, don't insert

Sits in existing slides 27–59 (Part 1's bucket-building through Part 2's Blue
explanation). Not a new section: the existing abstract board-work ("anything left of
this line is principal protected… CDs, money markets") happens to the Johnsons' named
accounts instead of generic categories. Same content, same order, same recovery-math
payoff — narrated, not re-derived.

The two worksheet passes are the one genuinely new addition, costing roughly 5–10
minutes of quiet in-room time.

**Untouched:** the tornado, Everest, the contractor, fiduciary vs. suitability, and the
close (113–117). The Step 2 form and the ~90% booking rate are not in scope.

**"What's My Gap?" deliberately left alone for now.** Structurally the same device, and
the Johnsons could carry it too, making them a throughline across the whole class. But
it sits after the two-hour conversion point the script itself identifies. Phase-two
idea, not designed here.

## One couple, not two

The DRA list runs $250k–$5M IPA — a 20x spread — and one couple's figures can't match
both ends. Sloan's position, which Isla agrees with: **identification runs on account
_types_ and life-stage decisions** — an old 401(k) nobody touched, some CDs, a rollover
nobody got around to — not on the total matching. A second couple doubles build time,
doubles the "wrong answer" moments, and dilutes the one clean disqualifier beat that
works precisely because it is singular.

The cheap fix for the spread is one spoken line: *"I'm using round numbers — the mix
matters more than the total. Whether you've got $300k or $3 million, the same three
buckets apply, just at a different scale."*

---

## The tornado — leave it alone

**Sloan declined an Isla suggestion here and was right to.** The obvious temptation is
to reuse "roof first, then walls, foundation survives" as the frame for sorting
accounts. Don't. That is a **survival** metaphor, and Green is the only bucket that is
survival-shaped. Blue's value proposition is *managed* risk, not immunity — folding it
into "what survives the storm" misstates what Blue is and manufactures a claim nobody
asked for. This is a compliance-shaped risk designed away from, not a taste call.

## Pilot vs. next class

**Sloan's answer:** pilot at Harrison, not NKU and not a new venue. Taleb's three

but the remaining risk isn't room composition, it's **execution seasoning.** The two
hours this touches are a script Justin has run for years and refined against real
faces. The Johnsons' version has none of that mileage on day one.

### ISLA'S AMENDMENT 2 — that pilot costs ~$10,000 nobody counted

Harrison has to be *filled*, and filling it means a mail drop — roughly $10k, per the
record, for 3 sign-ups. That is the most expensive possible way to season a format, and

**Use Sloan's own dry run as the pilot.** Free audience — existing clients, friends,
anyone who will sit for two hours. They won't convert, but conversion isn't what's
being tested; pacing and the feel of the room is. Season it for nothing, then put it in
front of a paid room.

## Compliance-shaped risks — flagged, NOT resolved

1. **The "not a real couple" disclaimer needs Ed's exact phrasing.** Realistic
   hypothetical account numbers on a slide can read as a case study to someone
   skimming, even when spoken as fiction.
2. **Beat 8 only holds if delivered as scripted.** Any ad-libbed "most people find…"
   recreates the population claim Ed has cut three times.
3. **The worksheet's no-verdict design needs the same review Ed applies to the mirror
   card** — same failure shape (Q5, the FB quiz's "Snapshot"), different artifact.
4. **The disqualifier beat carries the same Rule 35b-1 / recovery-math sensitivity**
   already flagged on slide 53. Relocating it onto a fictional couple doesn't remove
   that; it moves where Ed needs to look.
5. **Amendment 1's distinction** — neutrality runs on risk tolerance, not bucket mix —
   should be in front of Ed explicitly rather than left for him to infer.
