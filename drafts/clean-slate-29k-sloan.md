## 2. Your three positions, attacked

I read these as though I meant to reject them.

### 2.1 "More, smaller, cheaper rooms." Right objective, wrong cost model, and already on record.

- **It is already on record.** `reference/class/README.md` says "Small rooms are fine. Expensive rooms are not," and "volume was never the thing to optimise." The metric is already cost per booked meeting. What is new is "twelve rooms," and that is the weak part. (Pre-flight 6.)
- **The room was never the expensive part. The body is.** Everything in a drop that is not print and postage (list, design, venue, materials combined) was about $1,937 (`offer-options-and-ranking.md`). The recent Ohio drops ran about $2,850 per body (7 bodies, ~$20k). Twelve rooms of three is 36 bodies, which is on the order of **$100,000** at today's response, against a $29,000 budget. "Twelve near-free rooms of three beat one $10,000 room of three" holds only by assuming the fill away.
- **A room costs Justin about 4.5 hours whether two people come or twelve.** Twelve rooms is 54 hours. Under the power-law reading that is acceptable. It is acceptable only if someone is in the chairs, and the chairs have no source except mail, which is the $100k problem above.
- **Where the idea survives:** there are three cheap sources of a body. They are inbound from an owned asset, the warm list (already sat; it is a lab, not a source), and borrowed rooms that forbid Step 2. Only the first is real. So position 1 is not a separate strategy. **It is a consequence of position 3.** I keep the objective (shots, not bodies) and replace "twelve rooms" with "convene at two." I will not call that a breakthrough.
- **Unverified and load-bearing:** I cannot tell whether the $5M household's room cost $10k to fill. If it did, a "cheap two-attendee room" was a $5,000-a-head room that got lucky. Justin knows. See section 11.

### 2.2 "Spend on production and buy back Justin's hours." Half right, and the wrong half is the headline.

- **Right:** money cannot buy Justin more hours, but it can stop spending them on work that is not on camera. My estimate is that a 10-minute video takes about 8 Justin-hours unaided (scripting review, shooting, editing, thumbnail, title, upload) and about 1.75 with an editor and a packager. That is roughly 6 hours bought back per video at about $275 each, so **about $45 per Justin-hour bought back.** All numbers in this paragraph are my guesses (E); Q4 will measure them.
- **Wrong, or at least unproven:** "If YouTube stays time-funded it will lose every week to client work, forever." As of the last record (9/25) **no video has been shot.** That is a prediction with zero observed weeks behind it. (Pre-flight 5.)
- **What money does not buy is the stall point.** The stall is likely the shoot day, and Ed-then-Justin sign-off. The graduation checklist has an unchecked box for a piece going Sloan, Ed, Justin, published without Isla chasing; that pipeline has never run end to end. An editor does not fix either stall.
- **What money can buy is a commitment device.** A paid person on a booked shoot date, with a cancellation cost, converts an intention into an appointment. That is the only way money fixes the shoot-day failure. It is U2, and it is the least conventional line in this plan.
- **Camera setup:** I cut Isla's "camera setup" to $1,000 of kit. A whiteboard talk for a 55-64 audience is won on audio and clarity, not 4K. "Boring" is Justin's own word.

### 2.3 "Video can manufacture 'already begun.'" The strongest case for the land, and the least supported.

- **Why it is the right case:** it explains why video could reach past a 10-mile room and past the gatekeeper problem, which "it translates to the class" does not.
- **The evidence is an inference.** Taleb marked it as one: "an inference, not a finding." The nearest data point, the Facebook quiz that got "a lot of hits" and zero bookings, points the wrong way. I agree with the record that it is uninterpretable. It is still the only evidence, and it is not supportive.
- **The form has two parts, and video transfers only one.** Part one is continuity ("the next step of something you already began"). Part two is the physical, social, in-the-moment act: a menu of eight dated slots, "circle your top 2," a pen, ninety seconds after the content ends, in front of other people. A viewer at home has the content and none of the act. Watching six videos is also a much cheaper sunk cost than giving up a Saturday morning.
- **So the course needs a finish line, and the ask goes at the finish line.** A numbered course ("Part 5 of 12") makes "begun" countable. The ask comes at the end of the course, with a real slot menu and "included, no cost," in Justin's own Step 2 language. It does not go on every video. I would rather get zero asks early than train viewers to scroll past one.
- **A gate I cannot clear and Ed must:** ending a video with a free hour with an advisor is an offer of advisory time, not education. `CLAUDE.md` says that if a piece ever offers advisory services directly, re-ask Portfolio Medics. Without that answer the course has no conversion step, and the land thesis has no signal that counts.
- **Honest limit on what I would learn:** the people who will watch twelve chapters of a retirement course are self-educators, which is the class-shaped population the positioning statement was derived from. Video will be tested on the people it was derived from, so it cannot tell us about the delegator (limit 2 in `brand-voice.md` stays open). It can tell us whether video is a cheaper way to reach class-shaped people than mail, and the plan answers only that.

### 2.4 The one thing the power-law finding changes that none of the three says

The record (9/23) already says the DRA list spans a 20x range in household assets and every piece has treated it as one audience. If value is power-law, **spend per household should scale with expected value, not be flat per piece.** That is a cold-mail design question, so it is Jayme's and Taleb's, not mine, and I am not designing it. ## 3. The funding condition, re-argued

The condition (`ROADMAP.md`: "funded by time, never by the mail budget; one asset, boring, for two years") did two jobs.

**My verdict: yes, the unrestricted money should fund production, but not the way the condition is being argued.**
