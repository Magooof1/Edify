### Item 4. Interviewing the warm list

**What makes the interviews safe to run:**

1. **A one-paragraph purpose note in his own file before the first call**, saying this is research on why the mailer frame did not land, no offer is made, and nothing is kept for marketing.
2. **Research only, no offer on the call.** No product, no "while I have you." If the call turns into an offer, it is a different call: log it and treat it as outreach. Pure research calls fall outside the telemarketing rules and an offer does not [LAW, my understanding, counsel to confirm]. Established-relationship windows for people who only attended a class may be short, so no-pitch also protects here.
3. **No incentive of value.** A gift card is compensation to someone who may later be quoted, and for insurance clients it counts toward the rebating cap. A handwritten thank-you with no value is fine.
4. **Do not record**, or record with consent stated on the recording. Handwritten notes. Notes go in Trulip's file, **never in this repo's memory**; the hard rule on client data applies.
5. **One log line per interview** (date, who, how contacted, research-only, no offer made). This is the informal-channel capture step from the 2026-08-25 precedent.
6. **The two groups stay separate** (non-converters and clients who arrived without a class). With clients, say that answers do not affect service.
7. **Opening line:** "This isn't a sales call. I'm not going to offer you anything. You can stop anytime."

**What makes a quote usable or not:**

- **Default: research, not copy.** The insight can enter copy as Justin's own first-person observation, unattributed, with no population claim. "Six of the eight people I talked to..." is a statistic from n=8, and it falls under the 2026-09-19 ruling that a first-person hedge does not cure a population claim.
- **A verbatim quote in marketing is a testimonial-grade item**, whether or not the speaker became a client. A non-client's quote may not fit the rule's literal definition, but I would not rely on that: fair-and-balanced still applies, the state insurance ad rules on testimonials may apply [UNVERIFIED], and an attributed quote from a named prospect is a privacy problem.
- **To use one, consent comes first, in writing, as a separate opt-in. Never discovered afterwards** (2026-08-25 precedent, item 3). The speaker approves the exact text. The ad carries the disclosures (client status, compensation, conflicts). Keep the consent and the original notes. No cherry-picked pile of flattering lines. Plan for withdrawal.
- **Composite or "typical" quotes presented as real statements: closed.**
- The 2026-09-25 flag stands: "clients tell me they wish they'd learned this sooner," or any paraphrase attributed to clients, is a testimonial. The insight is free; the attributed quote is not.

**Cost:** ~30 minutes to set up, 4-6 hours of calls, $0. **Ranking: buy. It is the best free item in the file.**

### Item 6. Client appreciation and gift-shaped spending

- **Ohio:** S.B. 256, effective 21 July 2022. Non-cash gifts, items, services, meals or charitable donations in connection with marketing, sale, purchase or **retention** of insurance contracts, cost not exceeding **$250 per policy year or calendar year.** One source says that for personal lines the amount is set by the superintendent. Read ODI Bulletin 2022-04 and R.C. 3901.21 / 3901.213 before relying on the figure.

**Three properties matter:**
- "Retention" is in scope, so an appreciation gift to existing annuity clients counts.
- The cap is per recipient per year, so holiday gifts and every event add up.
- A service below market value counts as a gift of the difference.

## 6. Recordkeeping load at volume
