## 0. Who this is for, and why the cadence is shaped differently than the quiet track

**Audience:** roughly 100 households who came to one of Justin's classes — or signed up
for one — and never sat down for a one-on-one Financial House Analysis (FHA). This is a
different starting point than the ~30-household quiet track: those households have a
plan and went quiet on it. This group never had a plan built at all. They have the class,
not the picture.

That changes what the first touch has to do. It is not a follow-up on a plan — there is
no plan yet. It is a second invitation to the thing they came for: the free, one-on-one
version of what they saw done generally in the room. Say that plainly, on the first
touch, and don't dress it up as anything else.

**Ground rules carried forward from the quiet track, unchanged:**
- Every touch gives something before it asks for anything; give-touches outnumber
  ask-touches (3 give : 1 ask per year here, same ratio as the quiet track's Phase 2).
- No touch claims what "most people" think, find, or do. No touch implies urgency, a
  guaranteed outcome, or that not acting has a cost.
- Justin sends every one of these personally, copy-paste-and-fill-in-the-name. Not
  automated.
- Two-hat disclosure (Portfolio Medics for advisory, Trulip for insurance/planning) on
  every templated written touch. CAN-SPAM footer (unsubscribe + physical address) on
  every email. **No SMS in this track, full stop** — unlike the quiet track, there's no
  conditional "once consent is confirmed" here; the personal-note touch below is always
  a handwritten card, never a text. The phone call is the only footer-free touch, same
  Marketing Rule live-oral exclusion Ed applied to the quiet track's Touch 3.
- Off-ramp after 18 months of zero response, translated below for a cadence that's
  quarterly from day one (see section 3).

**One difference from the quiet track worth naming up front:** this audience already sat
in a room where the three buckets were taught by name. Per `.claude/memory/brand-
voice.md` (2026-09-16), bucket names are fair game with a warm audience — the class
installed the frame, so "Blue" isn't jargon to them, it's a callback. I've used the names
where they do real work (mostly Touch 1 and the quarterly market note) and left them out
where the mirror's own cleared language already does the job without a name (Touch 2).
Not a slogan — a shorthand this specific room already owns.

## 1. The shape of the cadence

No Phase 1/Phase 2 split. There's no "day 0 of a completed plan" to reinforce here, so
the cadence is quarterly from the moment a household is identified as "attended or
signed up, never met." **Four touches a year, one per quarter, indefinitely, until the
off-ramp or a booked FHA moves the household to a different track (they're not
"never-met" anymore).**

| Touch | Cadence slot | Medium | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Touch 1 — "The analysis you never got" | Q1 | Email | **Ask** |
| Touch 2 — The two-minute self-check | Q2 | Email | Give |
| Touch 3 — A quiet quarter, the bucket worth a second look | Q3 | Email | Give |
| Touch 4 — "No agenda" call | Q4 | Phone | Give |

One ask, three gives, same 3:1 ratio the quiet track settled on for its sustain phase —
and the same reasoning: the ask only works because it's earned by three touches that
don't ask for anything.

**Counting the touches:** 100 households x 4 touches/year = 400 touches/year. 400 / 52
weeks = **~7.7 touches/week, call it 8 a week** across the full pool — this matches the
brief's target directly, and it's a cleaner number than the quiet track's because there's
no front-loaded Phase 1 burst to smooth out; every household is on the same flat
quarterly rhythm from day one.

## 3. The off-ramp

Same standard as the quiet track — continuing to "give" at full rate to someone who's
never engaged reads as noise, not generosity — translated for a cadence that's quarterly
from day one rather than a 90-day Phase 1 followed by quarterly sustain:

**If a household has had zero response — no reply, no call answered, no click — across
six consecutive quarterly touches (about 18 months), it drops to once a year: Touch 1
(the ask) only, no quarterly gives.** I picked six touches rather than the quiet track's
four because this track has no separate onboarding phase to count against the 18-month
window — the quiet track's four quarterly touches sit on top of an initial 90-day burst,
so its "four touches" and this track's "six touches" both land at roughly the same 18
months elapsed. Worth Isla or Ed's eyes on whether that translation is the right one,
but I didn't want to silently shorten it to four touches (12 months) just because the
number looked cleaner.

A household that responds at any point — a reply, a call, a booked FHA — either resets
the clock to full quarterly rhythm, or, if the response is an actual booked Financial
House Analysis, graduates the household out of this track entirely and into whichever
track fits a household with a completed plan (the quiet track, if they don't convert to
a client from that meeting).

## 4. Compliance flags for Ed (not resolved here)

1. **Reused mirror copy in a new segment.** Touch 2 is the identical Q1-Q5/scoring
   content already cleared for the quiet track's Q-Give-2 and the correction email. Per
   Ed's own standing rule, a new segment is a new context even with zero copy changes —
   flagging for its own quick look rather than assuming the prior clearance travels.
2. **Bucket naming for this specific document.** Touch 1 and Touch 3 name the Blue
   bucket, consistent with `.claude/memory/brand-voice.md`'s ruling that names are fine
   with warm, in-class audiences. Ed previously confirmed naming is orthogonal to
   compliance (triggers attach to what's described, not what it's called) for the class
   context generally — flagging so that confirmation is read against this specific
   piece, not assumed to auto-apply.
3. **Disclosure sizing on Touch 3.** I gave it the full footer because it names a bucket
   feature (the downside plan); if Ed judges that describable more lightly, that's his
   call to shorten, not mine to have guessed at.
4. **No SMS, no exceptions, in this track.** Unlike the quiet track, there's no
   conditional path to text here — every personal-note-style touch in this cadence is a
   phone call (Touch 4) or an email, never SMS. If that ever needs to change, it needs
   its own TCPA consent-basis review from scratch, not an inherited one.
5. **Recordkeeping.** Same standard as the quiet track: per household, per touch — date,
   channel, which version was sent, any response — logged to Trulip's own Rule 204-2
   file, not this repo. The off-ramp and "graduate to a different track" events need
   their own log entries too, since they change which cadence a household is in.
6. **Scope question flagged above (Touch 1), not a compliance question:** the
   attended-vs-signed-up-only split within the ~100, and whether Touch 1 needs a second
   variant for the signed-up-only subset. That's a fact Isla/Justin have, not one I do.

## 5. My honest read

This is the easier build of the two tracks, and I'd say so even if it weren't true —
the math is genuinely cleaner (flat 4-a-year, no front-loaded phase to reason about),
and the story is more honest than it might first look: leading with "you never got your
own plan" isn't a weaker ask than the quiet track's touches, it's a clearer one. These
people already paid the only cost that matters — they showed up once. Re-inviting them
plainly is Godin's whole logic: permission already given once doesn't expire just because
they didn't act on it immediately.

Two things I'd push on if this were mine to decide alone, not just draft:

**First, don't let Touch 1 do double duty as both the entry point and the annual ask
forever.** As written, every household's "year" starts with the invitation and ends with
a call, then repeats. That's fine once or twice, but by year three, "you never got your
own plan" starts to read less true the longer someone's been in this pipeline without
acting — eventually the honest version of that line changes ("we've stayed in touch for
two years and the offer's still open" is a different, truer sentence than "you never got
your own plan"). I'd want a variant of Touch 1 for households past year one, not the
identical line reused indefinitely — flagging it here rather than quietly building it,
since it's beyond what was asked.

**Second, the 100-household number deserves a harder look than I can give it here.**
At 8 touches/week this fits easily alongside the quiet track's load and Justin's client
work — the arithmetic works. But "roughly 100" sitting in one bucket with no track at all
until now is itself worth Isla flagging upward: that's 100 people who showed up once and
got nothing back until today. If that number is actually growing faster than 8/week can
absorb (every new class adds to it), the cadence needs revisiting before it's built, not
after it's already straining.

Nothing here is a reason not to build it — it's the right next track, and the ratio and
frequency match what was asked. These are just the two things I'd flag rather than build
around quietly.
