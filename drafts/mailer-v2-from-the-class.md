## The one-line case

The rejected card opened with the *conclusion* of a twenty-slide argument (three
buckets, a disqualifier). This piece opens with the material Justin uses on people
who have never heard of him — the correction, the coordination question, the offer
in his own words — and saves the taxonomy for the one place a stranger has already
agreed to spend real attention: the landing page behind the QR code.

---

## Format

**One 8.5×11 sheet, bi-folded to 8.5×5.5. Four panels — cover, correction, offer,
disclosures-only.** Same physical spec Ed already worked out for the mirror card
(`drafts/mirror-card-v2.md`, note 6) and for the same reasons: the disclosure block
runs 300+ words and shouldn't compete for room or get shrunk to fit, a tri-fold
cramps whichever panel is carrying real copy, and one folded sheet stays a single
artifact for recordkeeping. Self-mailer, no envelope — at $1.00–1.50 all-in per
piece an envelope-plus-insert package isn't in the budget, and nothing in this
concept needs a teaser envelope to work (the cover panel does that job in the open).

**Production note, not mine to resolve:** the cover panel doubles as the address
panel on a self-mailer. High Note will need to confirm how much of the hook survives
next to postal indicia and the address block before this is locked — that's a
production conversation with them, not a copy decision, and per standing rule I'm
not contacting them without Justin confirming first.

## PANEL 1 — Cover (the three-second panel)

**Photo:** `justin-portrait-open-collar-hi-res.jpg` — the open-collar portrait, not
a tie shot. This is the piece's only face, and per `reference/brand-assets/README.md`
open collar is doing real work against the intimidation barrier on anything cold.

**Headline:**

> **LOOK ME UP BEFORE YOU READ ANOTHER WORD OF THIS.**

**Subhead, small, under the photo:**

> Go to brokercheck.finra.org. Type in Justin McGuffey. It's free, it takes about
> ten seconds, and it'll tell you things I don't have to.
>
> Then come back and read the rest.

**Why this is the cover, not a candidate for somewhere inside.** It is the only
device in the whole class that does the cover's one job and the trust job at the
same time. Every other asset — the tornado, Everest, the contractor — is an
explanation, and explanations are what the *inside* is for. An instruction this odd
("leave this piece and go verify me before you keep reading it") is the thing that
stops a hand over a trash can, because nothing else in that stack of mail asks the
reader to do that. And it needs no institution behind it — it works precisely
*because* it doesn't borrow anyone's name. It borrows nothing. It just dares.

**Confirmed independently, 2026-09-22 — Ed.** Ruled clean while this was in
progress, landing on the same read: the *instruction* to look him up is the safe
form; the piece must never reproduce his actual BrokerCheck record, since a
screenshot goes stale and reproducing a regulator's record risks implying
regulatory endorsement, which the Marketing Rule specifically prohibits. This panel
already only carries the instruction — no screenshot, no reproduced record — so it
stands as drafted.

## PANEL 2 — The correction (what the tornado and the contractor are for)

**Headline:**

> **You have a tax person. Maybe an insurance person. A 401(k) somewhere. Do any
> of them talk to each other?**

**Body:**

> If you were building a house, you'd hire a contractor — not because you can't
> find a plumber or an electrician on your own, but because somebody has to
> coordinate them. Otherwise the drywall goes up before the wiring's done.
>
> Most people are their own contractor when it comes to retirement. You probably
> picked your own investments inside your 401(k). Maybe you've got someone for
> insurance, someone for taxes. Good people, most of them. But is anyone actually
> coordinating what they're doing with what you're doing?
>
> Here's the question that matters: when you take Social Security, does your tax
> person know? When money comes out of an account, does whoever's managing it know
> whether that's going to be taxed? If the honest answer is "I'm not sure" —
> that's not a knowledge problem. It's a coordination problem.

**Small photo callout, bottom of panel:** `justin-whiteboard-three-buckets.jpg`,
caption: *"This is what that conversation looks like when it's three and a half
hours long. The couch version takes about an hour."*

**Flag for Ed on the photo.** Brand-assets README already raised this: the three
circles in that photo are unlabeled, and per the 2026-09-12 precedent ("triggers
attach to what's described, not named") an unlabeled colored ring probably isn't
"describing Green." Probably. Route it before it ships, not after — Ed's call, not
mine or a designer's.

**Ruled in bounds, 2026-09-22 — Isla, on Sloan's warm/cold boundary.** Sloan's
position is that the class's teaching *arcs* (Everest with Brennan and the altitude

compressed analogy, derived from the arc, not the arc transplanted — in bounds. The
principle carries forward past this piece: take the fragment, not the sequence, and
re-derive for cold rather than lift.

## PANEL 3 — The offer, in his own words

**Headline:**

> **I'll tell you what you already have.**

**Kicker, directly under the headline:**

> **It's free. There's nothing to buy, and nothing to sign.**

**Body:**

> Not a class. Not a sales meeting. A visit — about an hour, just you, sitting on
> a couch, having a conversation.
>
> We'll look at what you've already got and tell you how much of it is green, how
> much is blue, and how much is red. No products. Nothing to sign. Bring whatever's
> most comfortable for you — even if that's nothing at all.
>
> If it makes sense to keep talking, we'll set up a second visit. If it doesn't,
> you'll still know more about your own money than you did when you walked in.
>
> It's held at our office in Crestview Hills, right behind Thomas More University.

**CTA block:**

> **See what you have before you decide anything.**
> → [SHORT URL — see instrumentation below]
> → [QR CODE — same destination]
> → Or call [TRACKED PHONE NUMBER] and just ask for a visit.

**Vocabulary ruling applied here, 2026-09-22.** Isla upheld Sloan's ruling that any
piece describing a bucket uses Justin's own labels from the Step 2 form —
Principal Protected / Manage Risk — Tactical Investments / Accept Risk — Buy and
Hold Investments — never invented substitutes like "fully exposed," which reads as
a judgment on a legitimate choice to an audience whose own advisor named
intimidation as the barrier. This panel doesn't trip that rule because it never
describes a bucket — it names three bare color words inside a promise. If this
piece, or any future one, ever needs to say what a bucket *is* rather than just
that Justin will identify it, his labels are the only acceptable language, full
stop, not a style option.

**Why "green, blue, and red" appears here with zero explanation — the separate
question Isla's ruling left open, still mine to defend.** This is the one
place in the piece the bucket words show up, and they show up only inside a direct
promise — *I will tell you your mix* — never as a taxonomy the reader has to
understand to act. That's a real departure from Sloan's mirror-card decision
(`.claude/memory/growth-channels.md`) to keep the color names off the card entirely
as unexplained jargon outside the class, and I want to name that tension rather than
bury it: she made that call for an instrument the reader has to *use* — this is a
piece the reader only has to *want*, and mystery is doing curiosity's job here
instead of costing comprehension. I think the difference holds. It's a judgment
call, not a settled one, and it's the item I'd want Justin or Ed to push on hardest.

## Where the mirror actually lives, and why

**Argued position: the five-question mirror stays off this card entirely and lives
on the landing page behind the QR/URL. This is one piece, not two, sequenced as
hook → correction → offer → (for whoever clicks through) diagnostic.**

Reasons:

1. **The cold piece already has three jobs — get opened, produce doubt, make the
   offer.** A fourth job (walk a stranger through five yes/no questions on paper)
   is exactly the "argument as layout" failure mode this role exists to resist.
   Four panels of scannable copy is already a lot to ask of three seconds.
2. **The mirror is structurally the Step 2 form**, per `reference/class/README.md`
   — a self-identification instrument that's been converting warm attendees at
   roughly 90% for years. It was never designed to be read cold off a card; it was
   designed to be filled out by someone who's already decided to engage. The
   landing page is the colder-audience equivalent of "already in the room" — someone
   who typed a URL or scanned a code has opted into the next two minutes in a way a
   mailbox recipient hasn't yet.
3. **It keeps the existing test plan's instrumentation intact.** Stage 3 (landing
   page reached) through Stage 5 (mirror completed) in `drafts/mirror-mail-test-plan.md`
   already assumes the mirror sits behind the URL, not on the card. This piece
   doesn't reopen that structure; it just changes what earns the click.

**The honest risk in this choice, named plainly.** The 2021 control's dominant
response channel was the phone number, not a URL. Routing the primary CTA to a
landing page adds a step relative to just calling, and a 50–65 audience that skews
toward the phone may simply call instead — which is fine (the phone path is tracked
too, per instrumentation above) but means the mirror may get less traffic than the
booking number does. That's not a defect in the plan, it's a real possibility worth
watching in the Stage 3 vs. phone-path numbers once this runs.

## The cold equivalent of "free, included as part of the class" — reconciled with
## the control's own mechanism, 2026-09-22

**Isla caught a real contradiction in the first draft, and it needed resolving, not
picking a side.** My mechanism read of the 2021 control named FREE in large type as
load-bearing — it kills the cost objection before the reader decides to care. This
draft's first pass argued the opposite: that "free" is the least differentiated
word in retirement mail and concreteness should replace it. Both can't be right
about the same reader, and I'd overcorrected — the fix isn't to drop "free," it's
to see that the control's FREE and this piece's concreteness are answering two
different fears, not competing to answer the same one.

**The control's FREE was killing a cost objection about the event itself** — is
this Saturday actually going to cost me money, the way a "free" dinner seminar
quietly isn't. That fear exists regardless of how small the ask is, and nothing in
this piece removes it just by shrinking the ask from a Saturday to an hour. So
"free" still needs to appear, plainly, and I was wrong to let it disappear from
Panel 3 in the first draft — it's now back, as a kicker line directly under the
headline: **"It's free. There's nothing to buy, and nothing to sign."**

**What the class's "included as part of the class" line is doing is a second, later
fear — not the same one.** It reassures the reader mid-visit that they're not about
to get pitched, using sunk cost from Part One as the mechanism. A cold reader has no
Part One to draw that reassurance from, so sunk cost isn't available as a device
here — concreteness is what replaces *that* specific job, not the word "free"
itself: *a couch, a conversation, no products, nothing to sign, about an hour.*

**So the resolved position: both survive, doing different jobs.** "Free" answers
"will this cost me anything," stated once, plainly, high in the panel, exactly as
the control did it. Concreteness answers "will I get pitched once I'm there,"
which the control's structure never had to answer because a university lecture
hall doesn't carry that suspicion the way a one-on-one office visit does. I'd want
the Stage 5→6 dropoff (mirror completed → meeting booked) watched as the read on
whether the concreteness half is doing its job; the "free" half is the control's
finding, not a new bet.

## Why this beats the control's approach without an institution to borrow

The 2021 piece's mechanism, per `reference/winning-mailers/README.md`, was borrowed
institutional credibility — NKU's Student Union, multiple sessions, a course
catalog's shape. This piece has none of that available: no venue, no host, no
university name. The substitute isn't a weaker version of the same move. It's a
different move entirely:

- **BrokerCheck replaces the university.** "Trust NKU" becomes "trust what you can
  verify yourself in ten seconds" — a credibility device that needs no institution
  because it hands the checking to the reader instead of asking them to take
  anyone's word for it, Justin's included.
- **The Crestview Hills landmark replaces the campus.** "Right behind Thomas More
  University" does the same grounding job — a real, locatable place — that NKU's
  name did for free, without claiming any affiliation with it.
- **The correction panel replaces the classroom's Socratic exchange.** Justin
  produces doubt live, in the room, by asking questions and waiting for answers.
  Cold copy can't wait for an answer, so the panel does it in one pass: state the
  question, let the reader answer it silently, move to the offer. It's a weaker
  version of what he does live — that's real, and it's the actual bet this whole
  piece is making.
- **The offer itself is cheaper than the control's.** "I'll tell you what you have"
  in about an hour is a smaller ask than a free 3.5-hour Saturday class, which is
  the entire hypothesis `drafts/mirror-mail-test-plan.md` already exists to test.
  This piece doesn't add a new bet on top of that one — it's the same bet, built
  from stronger source material than the taxonomy-led card was.

## What I'm least sure about, in order

1. **The BrokerCheck cover.** No piece in this category has ever asked a stranger
   to leave the mail and go verify the sender before reading on. It might be the
   most interruptive, distinctive thing in the stack — or it might just be friction
   nobody pays before the payoff exists to justify it. This is the single biggest
   bet in the piece and the one I'd want to see Stage 3 numbers on before I'd defend
   it a second time.
2. **Sending the CTA to a landing page instead of leading with the phone number**,
   given the control's dominant channel was the phone. Both paths are tracked, but
   the emphasis in the layout (URL/QR first, phone second) is my call, not a tested
   one.
3. **Naming green/blue/red unexplained**, against Sloan's own precedent of leaving
   them off the mirror card for the same audience. Defended above; still a real
   disagreement with a standing decision, not a clean extension of it.
4. **The correction panel doing in one static read what Justin does live across
   several minutes of back-and-forth.** I compressed it hard. It may need to be
   punchier still, or it may already be too dense for three seconds — I don't have
   a way to test that from here.
