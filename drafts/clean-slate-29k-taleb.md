## R2. The question: calm market, spend now or hold?

### The position

**Hold the margin, spend the evidence, and let a dated deadline, not hope, release it.**

Pay the rent you can prove (one spring drop, capped at the scale where the 15-month payback was actually observed). Spend the forced 2026 money now on the instrument that tells you whether the picture can be made to look broken without a market. Hold the rest of the 2027 money behind one rule: it fires on the correction trigger, or on 1 September 2027, whichever comes first. If it never fires on the trigger, it is a fall drop at ordinary returns, six months later than it could have been. That is the entire price of the option.

If forced to a binary between "spend all $29,000 at the 15-month payback" and "hold all of it," I reject both. The first extrapolates a payback measured at about $10,000 a year to $29,000. The second throws away the one number in this practice that has been observed in a calm market.

### The argument

1. **The 15.5-month payback is already a calm-market number.** The two recent drops (April 2025 and April 2026) are the base for it. So "spend in a calm market" is not a bet on a hypothetical. It is taking the observed floor. Isla's framing, "returns on all three are market-contingent," is true of the upside, not of the floor. I would not argue against buying the floor.

2. **The floor was observed at one drop a year.** The base is one $10,000 drop producing about one mean client (Justin's figure; one client, not an observed distribution, see flag in R4). Nothing observed supports the same return on the second and third $10,000 into the same households. Class frequency does not predict dollars on Justin's own data, and the list is the same finite DRA universe that eleven competitors and Justin himself have mailed for years. **The marginal dollar beyond about $10,000 a year has no demonstrated return. So holding it forfeits no demonstrated return.**

3. **The restricted money has no alternative use, which flips the usual question.** The right comparison is not "payback versus nothing." It is "which permitted use extracts the most from money that expires, per Justin-hour." The cost of an extra drop is Justin's hours, list fatigue, and compliance surface, not dollars. Dollars are free at the margin. That favours spending the proven line and not manufacturing returns for the rest.

4. **The option's price is small, so it does not need much to pay.** One base throughout: **per $10,000 of held spend, a six-month delay, ten-year gross horizon (Justin's own horizon), proportional uplift k (the trigger makes the marginal dollar work k times as hard).** Holding beats spending early when P(trigger fires in time) exceeds P* = delay / (horizon x (k minus 1)) = 0.5 / (10 x (k-1)).

   | If the trigger makes the dollar work this much harder (k) | Break-even P(trigger), 10-year horizon | Break-even P(trigger), 3-year horizon (Justin's break-even horizon) |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1.5x | 10% | 33% |
   | 3x | 2.5% | 8% |
   | 12x (the 2021-to-now ratio on Justin's record; confounded, see below) | 0.5% | 1.5% |

   **P(trigger), inferred, second-hand:** since 1950 the S&P 500 has had about 38 corrections of 10% or more and about 14 of 20% or more (a Stacker/KEYT summary of the standard tallies), roughly 0.5 and 0.18 a year. The 15% rule Isla set is between them, so I interpolate about 0.3 a year (mine, unverified). For the twelve months to the 1 September deadline, P is about 1 minus e^-0.3, or **26%.** At 26%, holding wins if k is at least about 1.2 on the ten-year horizon or about 1.6 on the three-year horizon.

   **Distrust that arithmetic.** It says hold wins at almost any k above 1, which is too easy. The soft spots: k has been observed once and contradicted once (below); the model assumes the held money deploys normally at the deadline; and if turmoil lifts *organic* referrals regardless of spend, then k on the spend is about 1 and holding is neutral minus the delay. That is a bounded loss. It is not a free lunch.

5. **The evidence that markets drive growth in this practice is one big point, one flat point, and two blanks.** I compare the S&P episodes to Justin's vintages. Dates and sizes are Isla's figures from the record where noted, otherwise second-hand search summaries or memory.

   | Episode | Shape | Next vintage in Justin's record (sampled AUM acquired) | Read |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Q4 2018 (about -20% in about three months, my memory) | fast | 2019: $1.475M (two-class) vs 2018 $0.630M (one-class) | Mildly above the 2023 two-class year. Class count confounds. |
   | Early 2020 (-34% in about 33 days, Isla's figure) | fast, with a pandemic | 2021: $9.130M. **2020 not supplied.** | The one large point. Confounded by first-mover timing and by COVID-era closure of competing seminars. |
   | 2022 (about -19% on the year, bonds down too, Isla's figure) | slow | 2023: $1.050M (two-class) | Flat. The hole Justin's sentence has to survive. |
   | Feb to Apr 2025 (about -17% to -19% on closes in seven weeks; search summaries, second-hand) | fast | **2025 not supplied.** April 2025 class: 4 attendees from about 10,000 pieces (0.04%) | If the mail was in homes during or just after that drawdown, that is a direct contradiction. Justin knows the in-home dates. |

   Two of four episodes have a vintage on record. One is large and confounded; one is flat. The two blanks, 2020 and 2025, are the two I most want. So I will not bet the plan on k. I bet a margin on it, and the margin has a deadline. That is why the held pool is $12,000 of $29,000 and not $29,000.

6. **Calm spending buys the instrument. Held money buys the exposure.** The durable asset the restricted money cannot buy is not purchasable at all. What is purchasable is a *validated, instrumented, Ed-cleared machine*: a mirror piece that has been read once, household keys, a response path that books. Entering the next correction with an untested piece, an uncleared message and a Calendly page that does not exist yet is how you hold a gun with no firing pin. The 2026 spend (R1) is tuition for that. Most of what "ready" means costs Justin's time and Ed's, not restricted dollars. See R3 for the parallel lead-time list.

The only instrument in this plan that tries to *make* the picture look broken without a market is the mirror (five self-scored bucket questions that show a reader a missing bucket). ## R5. Family Day, re-argued

The first pass called it "the one line never to cut" on the concentration case. That case is void. The re-argument is weaker, and I want that visible.

2. **The money has no competing use** beyond the rent line, which is capped by the list. Client appreciation is a permitted category and is indifferent to the market. The real cost is six Justin-hours, Ed's anti-rebating work, and compliance exposure.
3. **The mechanism evidence is thin.** The only support is stated intent: Cerulli, second-hand via Family Wealth Report, more than 70% of heirs "likely" to change their parents' advisor. Likely is not an observed rate. I have no evidence from Justin's own book that heir transitions are where households leave.
4. **The one place the new data touches it:** solo decision-maker households are about 45% of AUM and female-controlled about 27%. In a solo household the adult child is the next decision-maker with no spouse buffer. That is an inference, not a finding.
5. **Verdict:** keep at $1,500. Demoted from "never cut" to "first cut if Justin's hours are tight." It is cheap, permitted, and mechanism-unproven. I would not defend it further than its hurdle. A client session *during* a correction is a different and better-timed use of appreciation money, and the held pool already carries it.
