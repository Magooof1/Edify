## 3. Question 1 — the objective function

**The argument.** In a power-law book the sum is the maximum. Two households carry most of the value (by Justin's account; see 2b). The average is not a stable statistic here, and nobody can estimate it from n=2. Any objective written in averages (units, cost per unit, response rate) ranks channels by a number that one lucky draw dominates.

**Mine:** maximise **independent top-tier first visits per Justin-hour**, subject to a ruin constraint. Each word is working:

- **Top-tier.** The list spans 20x in assets ($250K to ~$5M IPA). The Step 2 hour costs the same whatever the household holds.
- **Independent.** Eleven advisors mailing one household are not eleven draws; they are correlated through the crowd. A draw from a place the crowd does not stand (a search, a name check, a family) is worth more than another from the mailbox.
- **First visit.** Step 2 is where the ~90% starts. Upstream of it nothing is value yet.
- **Per Justin-hour.** Justin's hours bind, not cash. A $5M household and a $250K household take the same visit hour.
- **Ruin.** Losing a $5M household costs more than any acquisition line earns in 15 months.

**Why not the others.** *Shots on goal* is the unit arithmetic with better manners: unweighted by tier and by independence. *Variance* is a property you accept to get skew, not a goal. *Time-in-market* is a means: assets that keep drawing while Justin sleeps raise the draw rate, but they are not the target. *Optionality* is the structure of the budget (tranches, a reserve), not the objective.

**What the unit arithmetic gets wrong for $29,000:**

1. It ranks channels by cost per *household*. The card and the 54-64 band (Levers 1 and 2) lower cost per mid-tier household. Correct for rent and irrelevant to the tail; they deserve a small share of the money, not the whole conversation.
2. It treats a 20x range as one population. The record already says this (`growth-channels.md`, 2026-09-23); nothing downstream acted on it.
3. It makes every visit hour equal, so cost per Justin-hour is invisible.
4. It demands a readable verdict. The tail is unreadable (table below), so the rule has to be dollar caps, gates and censuses, not statistics.
5. It counts acquisitions only. Losing a whale does not appear in cost per unit.
6. It treats the budget formula as a ceiling to spend into. A procyclical formula (budget falls when AUM falls) should be hedged with a reserve that fires when Justin's own observation says referrals rise: a correction.

**The readability problem, stated once.** Base: 2 large households in ~5 years of the class channel (the record's "~5 years"), so 0.4 a year; the window is Oct 2026 to Dec 2027, 1.25 years, so 0.5 expected arrivals at base. Poisson.

| Arrival rate vs base | Expected arrivals in window | P(none) | P(at least one) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1x (base) | 0.5 | 61% | 39% |
| 2x | 1.0 | 37% | 63% |
| 3x | 1.5 | 22% | 78% |
| 5x | 2.5 | 8% | 92% |

If this plan triples the whale arrival rate, there is still a one-in-five chance Justin sees none by December 2027. **No gate in the spend plan is "a whale arrived."** The rate itself rests on n=2 and could be off by a factor of three either way.

## 4. Question 2 — whales: targetable or only harvestable?

**Resolved: harvestable.** Targetable only in the weak sense of choosing the pond and the lure.

**The ledger.**

For "by-product":
- Both outcomes came through ordinary classes, with no whale-specific mechanism. Isla's counter is right.
- Targeting a household requires the list to *see* it. IPA on a purchased list is a modeled estimate (the vendor's method is not verified here), and the pull stops at ~$5M, at or below both known outcomes.
- The smallest room produced one of them. A rule that cancels small rooms would have deleted $5M.
- Nobody has ever tried to target the tier, so there is no trial to cite for targeting. n=0.

Against "pure luck":
- The one whale story on record is a **seeker**: a ~$5M client who "had been to many events" and kept saying "I don't know what I don't know" (`brand-voice.md`, 2026-09-25). One conversation, one man, and I cannot tell which of the two households it is. But if it holds, wealthy and unresolved plus time and anxiety is a person who *searches*. Seekers are findable by being where seekers look, not by interrupting. This is the opposite of the "fifth seminar" hypothesis I killed on 2026-09-12, which selected on attendance frequency from the mailbox. Here the selector is search behaviour, observable at the moment of the search. Hypothesis, n=1.

**On the 5% from Bhattacharya.** Second-hand (abstract via search; fetch blocked): 385 of 8,195 active customers of one German retail brokerage, about 5%, took a free unbiased-advice offer, and those who most needed it took it least. Three things stop it being a cap on Justin. It is take-up of an offer by the customers' own brokerage, a warm relationship. It is a rate per human-addressed offer, not per piece mailed: Justin's response is about 0.026% per piece, so 5% is some 190x higher and not comparable. And I saw no breakdown by wealth. What it does say is useful and unwelcome: the people with the largest unexamined gap are the least likely to walk in, so the whales who need the visit most are the hardest to *target*. That is an argument for the seeker channel, where the person has already walked in.

**What maximises harvest.**

1. **Number of independent draws.** Buy them where seekers look (search, name checks, Part 1), not where the crowd interrupts.
2. **Hold the room for one household.** Cancel only at zero. The record supports "hold for one household" if question 2c comes back "one couple," and at most "hold for two" otherwise. No minimum-attendance rule: it is exactly the efficiency that deletes the right tail.
3. **Leave the class's back half alone** until 2d is answered.
4. **Do not clip the right tail of the pull** without a reason. Ask 2e. A ceiling costs nothing to lift for a census.
5. **Tier flag and a personal response at registration.** Match every registrant to the list's IPA band. A top-tier registrant gets a call from Justin within a day. Cost: minutes. This is the *only* inexpensive form of whale targeting there is, and it is post-response.
6. **Retention and heirs** (R2). The value of an existing whale outlasts any acquisition line. Second-hand, direction only: Cerulli reports more than 70% of heirs likely to change their parents' advisor (via Family Wealth Report; "likely" is stated intent, not an observed rate).
7. **Test targetability directly, cheaply, once.** The only way to turn n=0 into n=300 is a capped census of the top tier (R3). That is the barbell instinct, kept in shape and shrunk in stake.

**The barbell instinct, answered.** Isla's shape is right and I would shrink the stake. A few hundred expensive touches at the top of the range is a lottery ticket with a bounded price, and I fund it at $1,800 up front and $3,000 more only if it shows life. Weight goes where n=2 came from (rooms, seekers, retention), not where theory points (targeted touches). Break-even economics for the lottery leg (base named):

| Touch cost | x card cost | Break-even response multiple, household at $1M IPA (4x floor) | at $2.5M (10x) | at $5M (20x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $6 | 11.5x | 2.9x | **1.1x** | 0.6x |
| $12 | 22.9x | 5.7x | 2.3x | 1.1x |
| $30 | 57.3x | 14.3x | 5.7x | 2.9x |

*Base:* the $0.524 card (Jayme, 2024 quote, 8.5 x 5.5) reaching a household at the list floor ($250,000 IPA) at Harrison's response, 1 attendee-unit per 3,866 pieces. The multiple is touch-cost multiple divided by value multiple, assuming value scales with assets moved and the close rate holds. *Read:* a $6 personal letter to a $2.5M household breaks even at about the response the mass card already earns at the floor, roughly 1 per 3,400. **Two warnings.** (1) A $5M household will not move 100% of its IPA, so every value multiple here is an upper bound; if they move half, double every response multiple. (2) At 300 households a break-even response yields 0.09 visits. The economics are favourable and the test cannot see them. That is why R3's stop-loss is financial, not statistical.

## 5. Question 3 — the land's condition, re-argued

**What the condition says** (`ROADMAP.md`, 2026-09-25): local, funded by time and never the mail budget, one asset, boring, two years. Justin's decision. I would change one clause and leave the rest.

1. **"Funded by time" is the most expensive funding rule available.** Time is the binding constraint. Cash is not. The condition's purpose was to stop the land eating the rent. That is moot: the restricted $7,000 cannot touch it, and the unrestricted $22,000 carries no standing mail line in my plan. So ring-fence the cash instead of banning it: a capped line, released in tranches, that replaces Justin's *hours* (editing, captions, thumbnails, the shoot) and never his face, never mail dollars. 4. **Decision aids go first, and they are the answer to "the land is the steak."** A delegator will not watch a long class. But a stranger who got a seminar mailer, or got *his* mailer, tends to look the sender up and check the thing before going. Content for that moment ("what actually happens at a free retirement class in Northern Kentucky," "how to check any advisor in three minutes," "what to bring and what not to") meets a person mid-decision. Competitors' mail seeds it. Eleven advisors mailing the same households is not only crowding; it is a supply of people about to search. I found one competitor listing (a Fort Mitchell firm advertising 2026 evening seminars with a meal; advertising, not data) and no advisor-local decision aid in one search. 5. **Mail creates the name search; the land answers it.** Every R1 and R3 piece will make some recipients look Justin up. Today they find nothing built for them. That is the one stage between "mailed" and "booked" that has volume, and it is readable in Search Console as branded-search impressions.

I add three instruments with volume: Part 1 completions, branded-search impressions, and the "how did you hear" field. Cash tranche 2 releases only if the Q2 gate passes. Time stays committed for two years either way.

**What would prove it wrong.** Editing fails to cut Justin's hours per finished video by half over the first three; Part 1 gets starts and no completions; or the audience does not watch (the second of the two wrong-ifs on record, still untested). Pew, second-hand via a search summary because the fetch failed, says 85% of adults 50-64 ever use YouTube. "Ever use" is not "watch a local advisor for 45 minutes," so that supports the platform, not the thesis.

## 6. Question 4 — manufacturing "already begun" at scale

**The mechanism and its threat.** Step 2 works because it is the next step of something begun (`reference/class/README.md`): no cost, "included as part of class," pick two dates from a grid in the room. Two independent reads, mine and Sloan's, agree that a cold card cannot borrow that and that borrowed venues forbid the step. The threat is **selection**: the ~90% may be a property of people who gave a room four hours, not a property of "begun." Nothing I can buy separates those two. The online Part 1 is the first thing that can.

**What $29,000 actually buys, honestly: not much of the mechanism.** Begun-ness is built by sequence. The spending is cheap.

1. **Part 1 online (U1).** The first 45 minutes of the class, which contain no product, strategy or number (`class-content.md`), and are the only part the record says works on strangers. The Step 2 page *follows completion*: "Part 2 is your free visit." Scale is free after the shoot.
   - **Design constraint from the IKEA-effect work (Norton, Mochon, Ariely 2012, second-hand): labour leads to love only when the task is completed.** Failed or abandoned tasks erase the effect. So Part 1 cannot be one 45-minute file people drop at minute 20. Build it as short chapters, each with a visible completion mark. Sloan's lane; the constraint is mine.
   - Replicate Step 2's friction removals online: a grid of dates, no callback, nobody to talk to. Endowed progress (Nunes and Drèze 2006, second-hand: 34% against 19% at one car wash, n=300) is a design lever for Jayme, low evidence.
2. **A physical kit, for the tier where hundreds of dollars a head is rational (R3, then U5).** The FIT-kit logic already on record, scaled by money.
3. **The room itself (U6).** Rooms Justin owns, so no venue forbids Step 2.

**What this does not do.** It does not reach the delegator who will not be taught. The mirror (R1) and the private first step (R3) are the only delegator-shaped tools in the plan. Part 1 is for people who chose to click.

**What would prove it wrong.** Twenty Part 1 completers and one or fewer Step 2 bookings rejects a booking rate of 20% or more at about 93% confidence (0.8^20 + 20 x 0.2 x 0.8^19 = 7%). That is not 90%, and I do not expect 90%: the room's number is a selected population. A third of that would still beat anything cold.
