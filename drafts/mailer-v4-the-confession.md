## The cover check, run first

**Does the cover tell a stranger what they get?** Two prior covers died on this —
BrokerCheck-first proved credibility with no hook; the tornado-adjacent cover posed a
problem with no named payoff. This one is built so the hook (the confession) and the
payoff (the worksheet promise) are both visible on the same panel, in the same
glance — headline does the pull, subhead closes the gap it opens.

## PANEL 1 — Cover

**Headline:**

> **You've probably had a dinner seminar show up in your mailbox. I used to pay for
> those.**

The first half is Justin's own class-opening line, verbatim, already Ed-cleared as
written. The second half is new — the inversion the whole piece rests on: he funded
the machine, and now runs its opposite. A confession, not an accusation. It passes
the boundary-map test cleanly (delete the counterparty's motive and see what
survives): it says only what Justin did, and nothing about what any advisor who
takes sponsorship money intends by it.

**Subhead — the payoff, doing the work the last two covers skipped:**

> *Now I run a free class where you sort your own retirement money — green, blue,
> red — onto one page. You keep it. No pitch.*

Concrete, an outcome rather than a topic, and answers "what do I get" in the same
glance as the headline poses the question.

**Argument for this cover, against two I considered and rejected:**

1. **Rejected (not killed, a real fallback candidate): the worksheet promise alone,
   no confession.** *"Sort your own retirement money into three buckets — on one
   page you keep."* Lower compliance surface (nothing about Justin's own broker
   history at all), passes the cover check cleanly on its own, and is arguably the
   safer control to run first precisely because it touches none of the ten items on
   the boundary map. I didn't lead with it because it's generic — it reads like any
   workshop mailer, and doesn't solve the actual problem that got v1 killed: nothing
   in it says *why this one is different from the dinner-seminar mailer sitting next
   to it on the counter.* But it's a legitimate control candidate, not a rejected
   idea — see the testing recommendation at the end.
2. **Rejected: "I was a broker. I'm not one now." (boundary-map item 1) as the
   cover.** Cleanest-cleared line on the whole list, but it's a category statement —
   it tells the reader what Justin *is*, not what's in it for them. Exactly the
   failure mode Isla's cover-check memo warns against. Better as the first line of
   Panel 2, where the reader has already been given a reason to keep reading.
3. **Rejected: the neutrality claim ("I won't steer you") as the cover.** It's the
   single most disarming line in the whole material set, but it's not cleared to
   print — Ed's hardest stop on the boundary map, blocked on an open counsel
   question about differential compensation. A cover can't be built on a claim that
   might not survive to print day.

**What passing the cover check rests on here.** The subhead is the entire payoff —
if a reader doesn't register "I get to sort my own money and keep the page," the
confession headline is just a curious sentence with nowhere to land. That dependency
is named again under "least sure about," below.

## PANEL 2 — Reason to believe

**Headline:**

> **Here's how I know the difference.**

**Body:**

> I started as a broker — under the suitability standard. Today I'm an investment
> adviser representative, held to the fiduciary standard.
>
> For about ten years I traveled the country, working alongside [over
> 2,000/thousands of] advisors at Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James, and banks. Part
> of my job was sponsoring advisor events. If the business didn't come, I couldn't
> sponsor again.
>
> I did the best I could as a broker — but the role only let me offer what was on
> the approved list, not necessarily what was best for the client.
>
> That's why I stopped running dinner seminars and started teaching a free class
> instead.

**108 words**, including the headline. Every sentence maps to a boundary-map item
already ruled on:

- Broker/IAR — item 1, near-verbatim.
- "Alongside advisors at Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James, and banks" — item 6, the
  exact phrase Ed flagged as the one a careless edit turns into a BrokerCheck
  mismatch. **Kept intact, word for word.** Do not compress "alongside... at" to "at."
- The sponsorship sentence — item 2, **Isla's preferred form**, not Ed's original
  rewrite: it states a consequence to Justin and attributes motive to nobody.
- The limitation — item 5, the cleanest item on the whole list, near as-written.
- The close — item 9 (doesn't run dinner seminars now, by choice) and item 4 (why he
  started teaching), compressed into one sentence to hit the word budget.

**The bracketed number.** "[over 2,000/thousands of]" is written to survive either
answer — if Justin spends five minutes documenting his basis (territory × call
frequency × years, per the boundary map), the precise figure is worth more; if not,
it softens to "thousands of" with no other change to the sentence.

**What I deliberately left out.** Item 3 (the BD trips) and the full text of item 8
("this is not that" — already spent on the cover) didn't make the cut. Panel 2 is
doing four jobs already; a fifth would either blow the word budget or thin the ones
that matter more. If Justin wants the BD-trips detail back, it's a clean add at
~20 words, but I'd cut it before I'd cut the limitation sentence (item 5) — that one
is the actual reason-to-believe; the trips are color.

**Total across all three panels: 230 words** (41 cover + 108 Panel 2 + 81 Panel 3).

## Compliance risks flagged for later — not reviewed, not handled

here. Flagging so there's a real starting list when it goes through:

1. **"I used to pay for those" is new to print.** I believe it passes the
   boundary-map test (motive deleted, only Justin's own action stated) but it
   hasn't had Ed's own eyes on it in this exact construction.
2. **The sponsorship sentence ("if the business didn't come, I couldn't sponsor
   again")** is Isla's preferred form, but the boundary map flags that the absolute
   version needs documentary support (an old non-cash-compensation policy) or
   softens to "as I understood the arrangement." Open, not resolved by using it
   here.
3. **Naming Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James in cold print for the first time** —
   carried over from v3, still unconfirmed whether the class-deck's Portfolio
   Medics clearance travels to a mailed piece.
4. **The bracketed "[over 2,000/thousands of]"** needs Justin's basis documentation
   or defaults to the softer form — same open item as the boundary map.
5. **The neutrality line (Panel 3) is explicitly not cleared** — blocked on the
   2026-09-23 differential-compensation counsel question. Cannot print as-is.
6. **"No pitch" on the cover is a printed representation.** The existing
   `drafts/no-pitch-policy-note.md` covers the one-hour office visit; it doesn't yet
   say anything about the class or the worksheet moment this piece is actually
   promising. That note needs to be extended to cover what this piece claims, or

7. **The worksheet's "nobody sees it, nobody collects it" design** needs the same

8. **Geofence, flagged not assumed.** Bare bucket labels with no description of what

9. **The class redesign itself is unpiloted.** Per `class-sample-couple-build-v1.md`,
   no dry run has happened yet. This piece invites strangers into a class format

## What I'm least sure about, in order

1. **Whether "I used to pay for those" lands as a confession or as jargon.** The
   whole cover bet is that a stranger, in three seconds, connects "sponsoring
   dinner seminars" to "the mailer I've thrown away a dozen times" without any
   further explanation. If "sponsor" doesn't read as "paid for," the inversion
   doesn't fire and the headline is just a mildly curious non-sequitur. I can't test
   that from here.
2. **Whether Panel 2's sponsorship sentence needs a translation clause it doesn't
   have room for.** Compliance-clean, but "if the business didn't come, I couldn't
   sponsor again" assumes the reader already understands sponsorship-tied-to-
   production as a mechanism. A retiree who's never thought about how seminar
   funding works may need one more clause to land it — which competes directly with
   the word budget Justin cares about.
3. **Whether printing the neutrality line at all, flagged as provisional, is honest
   enough on the page itself**, versus holding the whole panel back until counsel
   answers. I made the call to write it in because the alternative (a softened
   version) is worse than either extreme, but I'd rather Justin weigh in on that
   judgment than have it buried in a compliance flag list.
4. **Whether the offer solves the problem it's meant to**, independent of the copy.
   The word-count objection this brief is answering is real, but the bigger
   unresolved risk is that this mailer invites people into a class format
   (`class-sample-couple-build-v1.md`) that has never been run once, even as a dry
   run. If Sloan's seasoning run hasn't happened by the time this would mail, that's
   a sequencing problem no amount of copy work fixes.

## If the insider frame is wrong

I don't think it is — this is a real improvement on both counts that killed v3: no
third-party grief, and a genuine, compliance-cleaner payoff (the worksheet) instead
of an implied problem with nowhere to land. The standing risk named in the brief is
real and worth restating rather than assuming solved: this genre pulls toward
implication with every draft, because implication is the stronger hook and
"structural fact, no insinuation" is a harder, weaker-feeling one to write. I've
tried to hold the line — no sentence here asserts a state of mind about any
identifiable advisor, living or not — but that pressure doesn't go away because one
draft resisted it. Whoever edits this next should re-run the boundary-map test on
every sentence, not just the ones that were flagged this time.
