## The test — where "about me" stops being about me

**Delete the counterparty's motive from the sentence and see what survives.**

If a true, complete, useful sentence remains — describing only what Justin did and the
structural incentive he operated inside — it is still about him, and usable. If
deleting the motive collapses the sentence or defangs its point, then the sentence's
real work was accusing an unnamed-but-identifiable class, and that is **disparagement
wearing a first-person verb.**

**Unnamed is not unidentifiable.** "The advisors on that platform," "the advisors I
sponsored" — narrow enough in context to be a real target.

### 1 — broker then, IAR now

Drop *"I can explain if needed."* In this register it pre-empts a question nobody
asked — the same error Justin killed on the mailer cover (*"by saying look me up first
there is no hook"*). State it flat.

> *"I started as a broker — under the suitability standard. Today I'm an investment
> adviser representative, held to the fiduciary standard."*

Reuses the class script's own suitability/fiduciary vocabulary rather than inventing.
Best-substantiated item on the list: it is exactly what BrokerCheck shows.

### 2 — the sponsorships

As written it asserts what the advisors did with the sponsorship, not only what Justin
did. Ed's form:

> *"Part of my job was sponsoring advisor events — funded by carriers who expected a
> level of production to keep the sponsorship going."*

**ISLA'S AMENDMENT — Justin's own sentence is better than Ed's fix, on Ed's own test.**

Justin said: *"If they didn't write I couldn't sponsor again."* That describes a
**consequence to Justin.** No motive is attributed to anyone. It passes the test
cleanly, and it is sharper than the replacement. Ed stripped the advisors' motive but
moved a motive onto the carriers ("expected"), which his own test would also catch.

Preferred form, closer to Justin's words:

> *"Part of my job was sponsoring advisor events. If the business didn't come, I
> couldn't sponsor again."*

If the absolute version ("couldn't sponsor again" as a rule rather than an impression)
stays, Ed wants documentary support — an old non-cash-compensation policy if it
survives — otherwise soften to *"as I understood the arrangement."*

### 3 — the BD trips

Keep it to the business arrangement, not to what the BD's advisors did to earn it.

> *"Carriers pay to be the exclusive option on a broker-dealer's platform — and sponsor
> the trips that come with it. I ran that budget."*

**Don't name the broker-dealer** unless Justin wants to and can stand behind every
detail. Naming a real firm raises the accuracy stakes and invites that firm to ask what
is being said about it.

### 4 — why he started teaching

One fact to resolve first: is *"as a Broker"* literal (he held the registration) or
loose industry parlance? Item 1 says literal. If literal, the timeline must sit
consistently with item 1 rather than floating as a vaguer separate claim.

> *"I started this course to pass along what working from the inside taught me — as a
> broker, and later watching how advisors on the fiduciary side actually operated."*

### 5 — the limitation

Non-disparaging, self-focused, and the limitation described is public regulatory
structure (a broker's approved product shelf), not an accusation. Matches the class's
already-approved "We need brokers… they stay in their lane" register.

> *"I did the best I could as a broker — but the role only let me offer what was on the
> approved list, not necessarily what was best for the client."*

### 6 — the fifteen years

**Keep "alongside." Never compress to "at" or "for."** The script says *"traveling the
country working alongside advisors at Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James and banks,"*
which correctly signals he was not employed by them. A copywriter tightening this for a
mailer is exactly where "alongside" quietly becomes "worked at Edward Jones" — which
misstates his employment history and manufactures the BrokerCheck mismatch below.

**This instruction travels with the material into every draft.**

### 7 — the two thousand

Usable as bare background. **Not usable as the setup for a conclusion about what other
advisors offer** — that conclusion is already dead on the record (disparagement,
unverifiable).

Substantiation is the weak point: a decade-old wholesaler CRM almost certainly doesn't
survive, and a specific number invites "show your math." Either Justin writes down his
basis once, dated (territory size × call frequency × years), or copy uses *"thousands
of advisors across the country."*

**Isla's note:** the number is already in the class deck that went through Portfolio
Medics, so it has survived one review. Keep the precise figure if he'll spend five
minutes documenting the basis — it is worth more than "thousands."

### 10 — the neutrality claim, and why it's the hardest stop

An affirmative *"I won't steer you"* sitting on top of an undisclosed fact: Justin is
**compensated differently by bucket** — insurance commission on Green through Trulip
Investment Management, LLC; advisory fee on Blue through Portfolio Medics.

Same defect as the 2026-09-18 performance-data proximity ruling: a disclosure elsewhere
on the piece doesn't cover a claim made on another panel. **The qualifier has to sit
adjacent to the claim, not in the footer.**

> *"Whatever you land on — green, blue, or red — I'll implement it. I do get paid
> differently depending which one it is, and I'll tell you that too."*

Not cleared on Ed's authority — it is the *shape* the fix takes. The underlying
disclosure question is still open with counsel (compliance-precedents.md, 2026-09-23),
and **this item shouldn't print before that's answered.**

**ISLA'S NOTE — the compliance fix is better marketing than the thing it replaces.**
"I get paid differently and I'll tell you that too" is disarming in a way the bare
neutrality claim never was. It converts the liability into the most trust-building line
available. That happens rarely; don't let a later draft tidy it away.

## The register — how quiet the insider frame has to be

The genre works by **implication**: the reader infers wrongdoing the piece never
asserts, because implication is unfalsifiable and does the emotional work. That
mechanism is precisely what the Marketing Rule catches — an ad can mislead "taken as a
whole," through juxtaposition or implication, even where every sentence is true.

So it runs backwards from how the genre normally works: **state the structural fact
plainly and let the reader supply the judgment.** Credibility comes from candor about
Justin's own participation and constraints — his alone to tell — never from insinuation
about anyone else.

**Clears:**
> *"I spent years as a broker before becoming a fee-based investment adviser
> representative — and there's a real difference in what each one is required to put
> first."*

**Doesn't clear:**
> *"Brokers push what pays them, not what's best for you — I know because I used to be
> one."*
Asserts motive about an entire licensed class; unverifiable; comparative. It also
indicts Justin's own former conduct as bad faith, taking him down with it.

**Clears:**
> *"For years, part of my job was sponsoring events for advisors — funded by the same
> companies whose products they sold."*

**Doesn't clear:**
> *"Those dinners weren't free — the advisors who took them knew what was expected in
> return."*
Asserts a specific state of mind held by an identifiable class.

## Four things Justin must do himself

**1. Pull his own BrokerCheck / IAPD report — before deciding, not before publishing.**

*Isla's amendment to Ed's sequencing.* Ed said "before finalizing." That's too late. If
there is a disclosure event on the record, the entire frame is wrong — "go look me up"
plus "I was inside the machine" would actively hurt. **This check decides whether to
use the frame at all, not how to word it.**

Four things to reconcile:
- Exact date the broker registration terminated (U5) versus becoming IAR-only. Item 1's

  value of the four. A piece that says "go look me up" and omits something the reader
  finds reads as concealment even when the omission was innocent.

  "I was a broker, I'm not one now" story. Two stints or a gap means the binary needs
  adjusting.

## The standing risk of this format

**For whoever drafts from this, permanently.** "Structural fact, no insinuation" is a
weaker hook than "here's what they don't want you to know," and finding the strongest
hook is the copywriter's whole job. The danger isn't any single sentence — it's
**steady pressure across drafts to sharpen candor into implication.** The class script's
own "We need brokers" line works precisely because it refuses that move.

Belongs in Jayme's standing checks alongside the cover check, if this frame survives.
