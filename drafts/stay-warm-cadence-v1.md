## 0. Who this is for, and why the cadence is shaped this way

**Audience:** people who completed a free Financial House Analysis with Justin — they
sat across the table, saw their own money sorted into the three buckets, and did not
become a client. This is the ~$7-10M pipeline referenced in
`.claude/memory/growth-channels.md` (2026-08-25 entry). They are not cold. Per the
eval-form data in the same file, the moment that converts people isn't fear of loss —
it's recognizing the missing middle bucket exists and they don't have it. Most of this
audience has already had that recognition; they just haven't acted on it yet, or the
timing wasn't right the day they sat down.

That changes what this cadence should do. It isn't top-of-funnel education (they've had
the class). It isn't a sales sequence (nothing here pitches a product). It's **staying
visibly, genuinely useful to someone who already has the plan in hand**, until either
life changes enough that they act on it, or they tell Justin to stop. Godin's frame:
earn the next conversation by being useful in this one. Miller's frame: every touch
keeps Justin in the role of guide with a plan, never in the role of someone chasing a
sale.

**Ground rules that shaped every touch below:**
- Every touch gives something — a fact, a question, a piece of Justin's own attention —
  before it asks for anything.
- Only one touch per cycle is built to ask for a meeting. The rest earn the right to
  send that one.
- No touch claims anything about what "most people" think, find, or do. No touch
  implies urgency, a guaranteed outcome, or that not acting has a cost.
- Justin sends every one of these personally, in his own voice, in under a couple of
  minutes per household (copy-paste, fill in the name). This is not an automated drip —
  automating it would undercut the exact thing that makes it work, which is that it's
  actually from him.

## 1. The shape of the cadence

**Phase 1 — First 90 days after a household is identified as "took the FHA, didn't
convert."** Three touches, roughly 30 days apart. This is the freshest window — the
three-bucket picture is still recent, not yet buried under a year of other mail.

**Phase 2 — Ongoing sustain, quarterly, indefinite.** Once Phase 1 completes, the
household moves into a standing quarterly rhythm: one touch per quarter, four per year,
running for as long as the household stays in the pipeline. One of the four annual
touches is the one built to ask for a meeting.

**Standing trigger, layered on top of both phases, not replacing them.** The
correction-triggered email (`drafts/mirror-card-correction-v2.md`) fires independently
whenever the S&P 500 closes 15%+ off its trailing 52-week high, per the operational
rule already written into that document. When it fires, it counts as an extra touch for
whoever's in this pipeline and doesn't wait for their place in the Phase 1/2 clock —
per the existing rule, once per episode, not once per bad day.

**The off-ramp.** This cadence does not run forever at full frequency by default. If a
household has had zero response — no reply, no call answered, no click — across four
consecutive quarterly touches (about 18 months past the end of Phase 1), it drops to
once a year: the annual ask-touch only, no quarterly gives. This isn't a punishment;
it's honest recognition that continuing to "give" at the same rate to someone who's
never engaged is closer to noise than generosity. A household that responds at any
point — a reply, a call, a booked meeting — resets the clock back to full quarterly
rhythm.

## 2. Phase 1 — the first 90 days

### Touch 1 — Day 0-7 — Email — "The picture is still yours"
**Medium:** email, sent by Justin.
**What it's for:** reinforce that the plan they already received has lasting value, on
its own, with no ask attached. This is the generosity move — give the thing again, for
free, expecting nothing back.

> **Subject:** The plan we built stays yours
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> When we sat down together, we walked through three places your money can live —
> money that can't lose value, money still moving on its own from an old job, and money
> positioned for growth but with a specific plan for the downside. That picture doesn't
> expire. It's still an accurate map of what you own; only the market around it
> changes.
>
> I'm not writing because I'm checking in on a decision. I wanted you to have this in
> writing, in case the paper copy ended up in a folder somewhere: the plan is yours,
> whether or not you ever pick up the phone again.
>
> If you ever want to look at it again — updated for wherever your accounts sit now —
> the door's open. Same as the first time: no cost, no obligation.
>
> — Justin

### Touch 2 — Day 30-35 — Short personal note (text or handwritten card) — "A question worth sitting with"
**Medium:** text message or a short handwritten card, sent by Justin personally.
**What it's for:** give them something to think about, not something to answer to him.
Reuses one question from the mirror's five-question set (already Ed-reviewed language,
`drafts/mirror-card-v2.md`, Q5) — **flagging explicitly: reused language in a new
delivery context (a 1:1 personal text/note vs. the mailed card) still needs its own Ed
look before use.** Same reasoning Ed applied to the correction variant — a different
channel and context is a new fact pattern, not an automatic pass.

> Hi [First Name] — one question that's stuck with me since we sat down: right now,
> could you name one part of your retirement savings that's invested for growth but has
> a specific plan in place designed to limit how much it could lose in a bad year?
>
> No need to answer me. Just worth sitting with.
> — Justin

**If sent as SMS:** needs its own TCPA consent-basis check per segment before use, same
open item flagged on the mirror's correction variant — an FHA intake form is not
automatically consent to text. Default to the handwritten card for any household where
texting consent isn't already confirmed.

### Touch 3 — Day 60-65 — Phone call — "No agenda" check-in
**Medium:** phone, called by Justin personally.
**What it's for:** presence, not progress. A short call with no pitch and no ask, so the
relationship stays a relationship and not a mail merge.

> "Hi [First Name], it's Justin — no agenda, I'm just working my way through the folks
> I've built a plan with this year to see how things are going. How's everything
> sitting with you these days?"

If the conversation naturally turns toward wanting another look at the plan, that's
Justin's live judgment call in the moment — this script is a door-opener, not a
gatekeeper.

## 3. Phase 2 — quarterly sustain (ongoing, after day 90)

Four touches a year, one per quarter. Three give; one asks.

### Q-Give-1 — Market or season note
**Medium:** email.
**What it's for:** a genuine, current piece of context — not fear, not a pitch, just
something worth knowing. Two ready variants depending on the quarter:

> **If nothing notable has happened in the markets that quarter — the seasonal
> version:**
> Subject: A quiet market quarter, and what that's actually worth
>
> Hi [First Name], nothing dramatic happened in the markets this quarter — which is its
> own kind of useful information. A calm stretch is a good time to look at whether your
> own three buckets still match where you actually are: same job situation, same
> accounts, same plans for retirement? If any of that's shifted, it's worth five
> minutes to see whether the picture we built still fits. If nothing's changed, there's
> nothing to do — this is just a check-in, not a nudge.
> — Justin

> **If the correction trigger fired that quarter:** the correction email
> (`drafts/mirror-card-correction-v2.md`) *is* this quarter's touch. Don't send both —
> one give-touch per quarter, and the correction email already carries the weight of
> it.

### Q-Give-2 — The two-minute self-check
**Medium:** email.
**What it's for:** hand them the full five-question self-check as a stand-alone gift,
not a pitch — reusing the mirror's Q1-Q5 and scoring key verbatim from
`drafts/mirror-card-v2.md` in email form. **Flagging the same way as Touch 2 above:**
this is the base card's already-reviewed copy, redeployed in a new context (a
non-mailed, individually-sent email to a warm pipeline segment rather than a cold or
mailed audience) — needs its own short Ed pass on the redeployment, not just the
original card clearance. If Ed has already cleared the correction variant's near-
identical email framing (`drafts/mirror-card-correction-v2.md`), that's the closer
comparison to check against, not a fresh build.

> Subject: Two minutes, five questions — no correction, no trigger, just worth doing
>
> Hi [First Name], no market news prompted this one — I send this to people I've built
> a plan with every so often, because the honest answer to these five questions doesn't
> change much month to month, but how closely people look at it does.
>
> [Insert Q1-Q5 and scoring key verbatim from `drafts/mirror-card-v2.md`]
>
> No wrong picture here — just yours, again.
> — Justin

### Q-Give-3 — Personal note (repeat of Touch 2's format, different content)
**Medium:** text or handwritten note.
**What it's for:** the same low-lift, high-warmth format as Phase 1 Touch 2, so the
cadence has a recognizable "just Justin, briefly" rhythm running through it. Rotate
through the other mirror questions (Q1-Q4) here instead of repeating Q5 every time.

### Q-Ask — Once a year — Email — the one touch built to ask
**Medium:** email.
**What it's for:** this is the single touch in the whole yearly cycle that actually
invites them back to the table. Everything else earns this.

> Subject: It's been about a year
>
> Hi [First Name], it's been about a year since we built your plan together. A lot can
> shift in a year — a job, a balance, a tax bracket, what retirement actually looks
> like up close instead of a few years out. Want to put 20 minutes on the calendar to
> see whether anything about your picture has changed enough to matter? No pitch — just
> an update, same as the first time.
> — Justin

## 4. Counting the touches — for the scoreboard

**Per household, year one:** 3 touches (Phase 1) + 4 touches (Phase 2) = **7 scheduled
touches**, plus however many correction-triggered emails fire that year (variable,
event-driven — counted separately, not folded into the baseline 7).

**Monthly rate, per household, blended across year one:** front-loaded — 3 touches in
the first 3 months (~1/month), then 4 touches spread across the remaining 9 months
(~0.33/month). Year two onward (full Phase 2 only, absent the off-ramp): 4
touches/year, ~0.33/month, steady.

**Open item, genuinely needed from Justin/Isla before this feeds the Core Four
scoreboard:** I don't have a headcount for how many discrete households currently sit
in the $7-10M pipeline — that number is what turns "0.33-1 touches/household/month"
into an actual weekly count against the "Warm (25)" target in `ROADMAP.md`. If the
pipeline is, say, 40 households, Phase 1 alone (for whichever slice is newly entering
it) plus Phase 2 sustain for the rest lands well within a 25/week budget alongside
Justin's client and CPA/attorney touches; if it's 150+, the math needs a second look
before Justin commits to running it personally at this frequency. This is a sizing
question, not a copy question — flagging rather than guessing.

## 5. Compliance flags for Ed (not resolved here)

1. **Advertisement status.** These are personalized 1:1 sends, but they're built from a
   repeated template sent to a defined group of prospective clients — Ed should weigh
   in on whether that crosses into "advertisement" under the Marketing Rule's
   definition (a template used at scale reads differently than a genuinely one-off
   personal email, even when each instance is personalized by name).
2. **Disclosure.** None of the touches above carry the two-hat disclosure block. If
   Ed determines these count as advertisements, they need it (probably a short footer
   version, not the full block, given the personal/email format) — same two-line
   structure as the mirror card (Portfolio Medics for advisory, Trulip for
   insurance/planning).
3. **Reused copy in new contexts.** Flagged inline above (Touches 2, Q-Give-2, Q-Give-3)
   — Q1-Q5 language is already Ed-reviewed for the mailed card and the correction email,
   but redeploying it as a personal text/note or a non-triggered email is a new context
   and, per Ed's own precedent on the correction variant, needs its own look rather than
   inheriting the prior clearance automatically.
4. **TCPA (SMS touches).** Flagged inline — consent basis needs confirming per segment
   before any text goes out; default to the handwritten-note version where consent
   isn't already confirmed.
5. **Recordkeeping (Rule 204-2).** Every send, call, and reply in this cadence needs
   logging the same way the mirror's correspondence does — who got what, when, and any
   response. This is an operational build (whatever Justin already uses to track
   outreach), not something this draft resolves.
6. **"No pitch" as a printed claim.** Several touches promise "no pitch, no
   obligation" — same category as the mirror card's promise. It only survives review if
   it's operationally true, same note as the mirror.

## 6. My honest read

This play is worth building — it's the single highest-leverage thing on Isla's Stage A
list because the audience already did the hard part (they sat through the plan and, per
the eval-form data, mostly recognized the gap). The risk isn't the concept; it's
frequency creep. It would be easy to let this turn into more touches than Justin can
actually send personally, at which point either the "personal" part quietly becomes
automated (which breaks the thing that makes it work) or it stops happening at all
(which is the actual failure mode the Core Four table calls out — zero-input weeks).
I kept this to 7 scheduled touches a year on purpose, with an explicit off-ramp, because
a cadence Justin can't sustain for a full year isn't a cadence — it's a launch that
fizzles by month four. If Isla and Justin want it heavier, the honest tradeoff is either
more of Justin's personal time or accepting that some touches move to a lighter-weight
assisted version — that's a real conversation to have, not something to solve by
quietly padding the schedule.
