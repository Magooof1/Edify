## Slide 1 — "Planning and Coordinating Your Retirement"

Welcome to Planning and Coordinating Your Retirement. My name is Justin McGuffey and
I'll be your instructor today.

Before we get started, I like to always start by putting the course into context, tell
you a little bit about me, and tell you why we created this course.

I've been in the financial services industry going on a little over 15 years now. What
I did early on in my career is I traveled around and worked with different financial
advisors across the country — places like Chicago, New York, obviously here in Ohio and
Kentucky. Firms like Edward Jones, LPL, Raymond James, the banks. Pretty much any
financial institution you could think of.

Fast forward a few years — about ten years into my career — a family friend of mine who
was a bit older came to me and said, "Justin, I'm getting ready to retire. I know you
work in the industry. Is there anything you could help me with?"

It was a little bit of a tough question for me, because I wasn't working directly with
individuals at that point — I was working with financial institutions. So I said, let me
think about who I'd recommend and the criteria, and I'll get back to you.

At that point I had worked with maybe 2,000 different financial advisors. I went through
the different criteria of what I liked about what they did, what I didn't like. And it
was a very important decision, because she was a friend of mine and her husband had just
passed away and she was looking for some guidance.

Out of those thousands, I came back to her and there was only a handful I'd recommend —
that were up to my standard. It doesn't mean there was anything wrong with the other
people. That was just my recommendation based off everybody I had worked with. Some of
them focused on retirement planning, some did not. Some did investment things I wouldn't
recommend for people going into retirement, as far as risk is concerned.

**That's why we created this course.**

We didn't want to do — you've probably had a dinner seminar or something like that show
up in your mailbox. **This is not that.** The goal was not to sell anybody on anything.
The idea here is just to give you education.

We are going to go through a lot of material and we can't cover everything. I always
say: **we'll go a mile wide and an inch deep.**

## Slide 4 — Your Financial House (the master frame)

*The framework the whole course is built on. Taught as a question to the room, not as
a diagram on a slide.*

So what we're going to do is build your financial house.

**Has anybody here ever been through a natural disaster?** There were a bunch of
tornadoes that came through here in Cincinnati a few years ago, up in Dayton — and
there was a big one that came through my hometown in Harrison back in the 80s.

**What is the first thing to go on a house when you're going through a tornado? What's
the first part of the house that has a structural issue?**

It's the roof, right. Then the walls go. And what we have left is a foundation.

So the foundation is what we're going to start with, and we're going to build up from
there.

**Our foundation is going to be our money** — think investments, savings, 401(k)s.
That's what's going to support the rest of our house.

Your money can only go one of two places. It can either go **to you as income** during
your retirement, or you can **leave it to them as a legacy**.

Now, there is a third place the money can go. And where is that?

**Taxes, right.** So we want to avoid taxes as much as legally possible.

That's our framework for building your financial house.

> **Note for whoever builds from this.** The structure is taught by destroying a house,
> not by drawing one — the roof goes first, then the walls, and what survives is the
> foundation. That is why money is the foundation and why the course starts there. No
> marketing piece has ever used this, and it is the most memorable thirty seconds in the
> opening.

## Slide 6 — Mount Everest (the danger-zone frame)

**Anybody know what mountain this is? The tallest mountain in the world — Mount
Everest.**

Does anybody know **where most of the accidents happen? They happen on the way down.**
I'll tell you why.

There was a climber here in Cincinnati named Matt Brennan, on the news a while back,
training to climb Everest. You get your Sherpa, and there are only a few times a year
you can actually climb, because of weather.

The first time he goes out, he trains for months and months. Goes out, gets to base
camp one — gets hurt, I believe he hurt his groin, and had to go back down. Then right
around COVID, he couldn't do it. Then he did it again.

And he got to 19,000 feet and had to turn around. Because there are only certain times
of year you can climb, there was a bottleneck toward the peak. It was taking too long
for him to get to the top, and he had to turn around.

**Does anybody know why? He was running out of oxygen.**

*Justin draws a mountain on the board and works up the side: 45, 55, 65, 75, 85, 95 —
then circles the stretch between 55 and 65.*

I equate that to retirement. **Because where do most of the accidents happen?** It's
where you're making most of your decisions.

Most of your life, through your employer, you're getting health insurance — now I have
to make **Medicare** decisions. I stop working, so instead of putting money into my
401(k)s and IRAs, I'm **taking money out**. I have to make **Social Security**
decisions. Maybe I now need to look at my **wills and trusts**. **Pensions.**

All these different things you never had to do before, that you now have to do at one
time. **That makes it more dangerous. That's where most of the accidents happen.**

And that's part of the context of the course. What did I learn going out and visiting
those 2,000 advisors? Where did I see not only their clients make mistakes — but *them*
make mistakes?

I saw an advisor in 2008, markets crashing, complaining and yelling because his clients
wanted to get out, and he's saying *no, no, you've got to hold, you've got to hold.*
I've seen people make IRA withdrawal mistakes.

So that is the goal of this course. From my travels, from working with all those
advisors: **what are the mistakes I've seen people make — and my goal is for you not to
make those.**
