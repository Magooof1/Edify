## The whiteboard shot is the valuable one

`justin-whiteboard-three-buckets.jpg` — Justin at a whiteboard reading "PLANNING YOUR
RETIREMENT," a house drawn with **YOU** inside it, and **three circles beneath it in
green, blue and red.**

This is not a stock advisor photo. It is a photograph of his actual framework being
taught — the Financial House with the client inside it, and the three buckets in
their own colors. Most advisors have a headshot; he has a picture of his method.

Against the failures this workforce has already diagnosed, it does three jobs at once:

- **It puts a face on a piece.** The rejected three-buckets card had none, and a warm
  photograph of real people is among the load-bearing elements of the 2021 control
  that produced $6M (`reference/winning-mailers/`).
- **It shows him teaching**, which is what the education brand actually is and what the
  control's classroom photograph conveyed without a word of copy.
- **It carries the three-bucket frame visually**, with no claim made and nothing named.

**One caveat, and it is a real one — flag to Ed before this appears in any cold piece.**
The three circles are Green, Blue and Red. Green is principal-protected insurance, and
describing Green is what triggers the OH/KY insurance geofence. In this photograph the
circles are unlabeled — a cold reader sees coloured rings and no claim — so it probably
does not constitute describing Green. But that is Ed's call, not a designer's.

The principle is already on record from his three-buckets preflight: *a designer pairing
visual elements can introduce an implication "without anyone treating it as new copy
needing review."* A photograph is not exempt from review just because it is a
photograph. Route it before it ships, not after.

## Portraits

| File | Notes |
|---|---|
| `justin-portrait-open-collar-hi-res.jpg` | Highest resolution of the set (1.4 MB). Navy windowpane suit, white open collar, broad warm smile. The default for consumer-facing work. |
| `justin-portrait-open-collar-alt.jpg` | Same setup, tighter crop, lower resolution. |
| `justin-portrait-tie-a.jpg` | Red patterned tie, more formal register, softer smile. |
| `justin-portrait-tie-b.jpg` | Near-identical to the above; keep one, not both, in any layout. |

**Open collar over tie for anything cold, and this is not a style preference.** Justin
named intimidation on 2026-09-21 as one of the two things that hold people back from
working with an advisor — a status fear, distinct from the money fear, and one that
information does not cure. A tie reads institutional; an open collar reads
approachable. Where a piece is reaching someone who has not chosen to listen, the
register is doing work against that barrier.

See `.claude/memory/growth-channels.md` (2026-09-21) for the principle itself.
