## 2026-09-18 — The 60/40 disqualifier (Justin, verbatim in substance)

**What it is.** The classroom argument that dismantles the false middle bucket — the
moment attendees who believe they already have Blue (a 60/40 mix, "my advisor handles
it") discover they don't.

**Justin's words:**

> "When I'm talking about investments, I show them how a 60/40 mix — how much it went
> down in 2008, 2000. And I ask them: is that money that you would think would be
> managed, or is that money that's just riding the markets? Are you managing the risk by
> having 60/40? And it shows them — okay, well, 60/40 was still down, let's call it 20%
> in 2008 or 2020. It was still down significantly. So is that managing the risk? No.
> And then they realize: I just have bonds, and that isn't managing the risk."

**Structure (preserve this, not just the words):**
- **Socratic, not asserted.** Justin shows a historical result and asks whether it looks
  managed. He does not tell them their bonds aren't a plan — the attendee reaches "I
  just have bonds, and that isn't managing the risk" on their own. Any script or
  repurposed version that turns this into a stated claim rather than a question breaks
  the mechanism.
- **The binary is "managed" vs. "riding the markets."** These are Justin's own words for
  what Blue does vs. what Red does. A 60/40 mix, on this evidence, rides — it isn't
  Blue no matter how the attendee had been describing it to themselves.
- **The evidence is historical drawdown data for a 60/40 mix** — 2008, 2000, 2020 —
  with "let's call it 20%" given in class as an approximation for 2008. Any printed or
  recorded use of this argument needs the actual sourced figure, not the classroom
  approximation — that substantiation requirement is Ed's to enforce, not a copy
  preference.

**Why it matters (the false-positive problem).** Justin's own framing today: people
walk into the three-bucket picture already assuming they have all three — "I'm 60/40,"
"my advisor handles it" — and the three-bucket picture alone doesn't correct that; it
can let them wrongly check all three boxes. This argument is what actually breaks the
false middle. Justin also reported a client who had attended four prior seminars and
never once heard Blue described; whether that client specifically never heard the 60/40
argument is Isla's inference, not Justin's statement — but since this argument is how
Blue lands in the class, it's a reasonable read that the piece is differentiated
content, not generic retirement-class material.

**Where it sits in the class.** Comes after the three-bucket setup (Green/Blue/Red —
see CLAUDE.md for the mapping to hats) and functions as the mechanism that converts
"I already have a middle bucket" into the Blue realization that brand-voice.md
identifies as the actual conversion mechanism. Justin: "There's a lot that builds up to
it in the class."

**Standing note for content agents.** Justin's offer is open-ended — "I can give you as
much of the class as you want" — and this file is where future sections land as he
gives them. Isla has deliberately not pulled further on this yet (the fall card takes
priority), so as of this entry the 60/40 disqualifier is the only section on record.
When content work needs classroom logic beyond what's written here, the answer is to
ask Justin for that section directly, not to infer or reconstruct it.

## 2026-09-19 — Class logistics: schedule, audience age, and what's provided

Facts supplied directly by Justin, 2026-09-19.

**Audience: ages 50-65, not 65+.** A substantially still-working population, not
retirees. Downstream consequence for anyone modeling this audience: see
growth-channels.md (2026-09-19 entry) — the DRA list's age select should match this
range, and any prior content or targeting built against a 65+ retiree audience
should be rechecked.

**Schedule: Saturday morning, 8:30am-12:30pm — three and a half to four hours.**
Justin has trimmed the session from four hours to three and a half.

**Reconciling with the "40 minutes" figure elsewhere in the record.**
growth-channels.md (2026-09-12, Taleb's review of "Do you have Blue?") refers to
"the classroom mechanism (the 40 minutes that installs the frame)." That is not a
second, conflicting class length — it names the portion of the 3.5-4 hour Saturday
session that installs the three-bucket frame and the 60/40 disqualifier (above), not
the length of the session itself. Read "40 minutes" as a segment inside the Saturday
class, not as the class's duration.

**Provided: drinks and snacks. No meal.** Adds detail to, and is consistent with,
the standing brand decision that Justin has never run a dinner seminar and avoids
them deliberately (brand-voice.md, 2026-09-19) — this is what he provides instead.
